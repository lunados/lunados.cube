## 1. Abstract

AIAEAO records human and machine work so that **who did what, when, why, under which authority, with which result, on what basis, and with what degree of satisfaction** remains reconstructable.

**AIAEAO v9.3 retains a seven-coordinate description and navigation frame:** **X / Y / Z / T / F / M / O**. Only **X/Y/Z** are *primary ontology homes*. **T** qualifies temporal roles, **F** describes methods/functions/composition, **M** qualifies operating mode, and **O** describes observer perspective. Neither the four additional coordinates nor a qualified context automatically creates a canonical root, independent persistence domain, numeric metric dimension or execution permission.

The **integrated contract candidates** **MEAS-01** (historically M01), **FUNC-01** (historically F01), **REL-01** (historically R01), **TIME-01..06**, and the **PAT-* family** have distinct definition homes in the existing methodology / topology. This relocation neither promotes them to accepted production schemas nor creates new root types. The technical truth, identity, trust, authorization and information-loss limits remain independent.

The coordinates are a **closed vocabulary for navigation**, not a seven-dimensional Euclidean space and not a license to invent seven separate storage substrates. The previous X/Y/Z primary-home completeness invariant is retained.
| Axis | Question | Primary family | Object / expression family | Third family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **X — Dimensions** | What does it mean; what holds? | 𝝭 𝝘 𝝨 𝝣 𝝥 (Ψ Γ Σ Ξ Π) | Scope with one Key Pair per dimension | Logic Relations |
| **Y — Identity & Objects** | Who or what is identified? | Identity Anchors | Identified / Resource Objects | Relation Arrows |
| **Z — Materialization & Boundary** | Where is it embodied, situated, or bounded? | Module Bodies | Governance & Boundary | Resource Zones |

Every canonical element receives **one primary definition home** in X/Y/Z. T/F/M/O qualify, navigate or transform referenced material without changing its home. X/Y/Z are analytical ontology homes, not storage containers or execution stages. Typed relationships may cross them. A pattern, projection, context fingerprint or simulated proof never automatically becomes executable workflow, authorized action or accepted truth.

The five X dimensions are **𝝭 (Ψ) Epistemology**, **𝝘 (Γ) Governance**, **𝝨 (Σ) Semantics**, **𝝣 (Ξ) Execution** and **𝝥 (Π) Provenance**. Each frames one Scope and one Key Pair. Their detailed definitions follow in §6.2; their logical nesting is treated in §5.

The reading path is deliberate:

```text
§3 MORPHOLOGY      X/Y/Z homes + T/F/M/O qualifiers; α/β/ζ candidate layer grammar
§4 PHENOMENOLOGY   observer-relative appearance, context assembly and projection
§5 METHODOLOGY     X pairs + relations, type–circuit rules, comparison and patterns
§6 ONTOLOGY        one primary X/Y/Z definition home; proposed qualifications
------------------ CONCEPTUAL / TECHNICAL BOUNDARY ------------------
§7 TOPOLOGY        retained technical bindings + proposed REL/TIME/PAT contracts and tests
```

**Additional discipline after Experiments 010–012.** A typed path is not an inferred predicate; a certified statement is not necessarily true; a mathematically admissible formula is not a trusted measurement; a valid certificate is not admission. Experiment results remain conditional on their bounded synthetic generators, contracts and input facts.

**Core thesis.** Project is the administrative/ownership boundary; Thread is the semantic/provenance boundary; Execution Boundary constrains the effects of admitted activity. These are **Z-boundary concepts**, not additional X dimensions. The X pairs express knowledge, permission, intention, responsibility and occurrence; the Y axis identifies participants and things. No axis supersedes the others.

## 2. Terminology & Nomenclature

**Terminology** specifies meanings and conceptual boundaries; **nomenclature** specifies names, symbols, identifiers and aliases. Both must distinguish *established disciplinary terms* from *AIAEAO-specific restrictions*. Semantic roots remain defined only at their primary homes in §6; this glossary **does not** duplicate ontological definitions. Technical bindings belong to §7.

**Terminological discipline:** Prefer an established meaning when available; qualify a term when AIAEAO uses a narrower sense; remove ambiguous vocabulary from the active model rather than establishing accidental synonyms. Historical source language may remain in labeled historical appendices.

### 2.1 Reading Conventions

| Marking | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `code` | technical identifier, field, value or schema-level name; its binding is specified in §7 and frozen only with an accepted schema version | `event_id`, `causation_id` |
| **Bold canonical term** | defined at one primary home in §6 | **Conversation** |
| ALIAS | descriptive/app reader term with no right to redefine the canonical model | — |
| 𝝭 𝝘 𝝨 𝝣 𝝥 · 𝕬 𝕽 𝕻 … | Visual Grammar anchors; presentation handles, not technical authority | Ψ Epistemology |

A term is either **general** (part of this reference model) or **app-specific** (an alias mapped to a general term via Annex C). Descriptive family headings do not introduce new canonical entities.

| Naming layer | Responsibility | Home | Change discipline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory | distinctions, semantic boundaries, classification | §§2–6 | explicit model revision |
| Technical | identifiers, schemas, persistence, integrity | §7 | schema versioning |
| App alias | application-specific surface vocabulary | Annex C | flexible; one general referent |

### 2.2 Terminological Foundations and AIAEAO-Specific Terms

| Term | Established / general meaning | AIAEAO-specific restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Morphology | organization of forms/structures | coordinate and classification grammar, with X/Y/Z as ontology homes |
| Phenomenology | study/description of appearance from a standpoint | observer-relative, rights-/context-/time-qualified appearance (§4) |
| Methodology | system of methods and their justification | X-pair logic and typed, bounded compositional procedures (§5) |
| Ontology | account of entities/categories and their meaning | exactly one primary definition home in X/Y/Z (§6) |
| Topology | arrangements and connections (meaning depends on discipline) | **technical** bindings/persistence/constraints (§7), not mathematical topology |
| Dimension | general analytical, mathematical or physical dimension | **X-Dimension** means exclusively one of Ψ Γ Σ Ξ Π; not a metric vector coordinate |
| Scope | general extent/context of validity or consideration | **X-Scope** means one frame of one X Key Pair; *scope* elsewhere retains its general meaning |
| Coordinate | reference dimension or parameter in a description | one of X/Y/Z/T/F/M/O; does not imply orthogonality or numeric distance |
| Key Pair | no universal technical meaning | two distinct endpoints of one X-Scope |

**Terminology hierarchy:** *Terminology* defines meaning; *Nomenclature* controls labels and IDs; *Ontology* assigns one primary home; *Relational Grammar* defines admissible predicates; *Methodology* states compositions and decision procedures; *Topology* binds them technically. Generic `Scope` and `Dimension` remain available outside the narrow **X-Scope** and **X-Dimension** meanings.

### 2.3 Nomenclature Discipline

Canonical naming keeps meaning, boundary, identity, relationships and authority inspectable. Use one canonical term for one responsibility; state crucial non-equivalences; keep transport channels out of the ontology unless semantically independent; map foreign terms through adapters; do not convert unknown into known; introduce new terms only when an existing one fails to preserve a real distinction.

```text
Thread != Conversation != runtime session
Actor != Role Binding != Principal != Profile
Prompt != Role != Authority
Capability != Authority
Result != Outcome != Verification != Completion != Satisfaction
Artifact != Evidence != canonical truth
Relay Thread != Canonical Thread
Context != complete Thread state
Contract (assignment scope) != interface/schema contract
Phenomenology (appearance) != Epistemology (justification)
```

**Interpretation rule:** A cross-axis mention is a reference, not a second definition. A logical nesting in §5 does not establish technical containment in §7. An app-specific term cannot alter either rule.

### 2.4 Applied Boundary Examples

**`AGENTS.md` vs. `SKILL.md`.** Within the source repository, `AGENTS.md` is a scoped working agreement constraining responsibilities and approvals; `SKILL.md` describes a reusable method or capability package. Package presence alone proves neither activation nor compliance nor authority. Working agreements may constrain skill use; skills do not grant Authority.

**Prompt vs. Role.** A Prompt is a transient instruction/context payload. A Role Binding is contextual responsibility assigned to a stable Actor within a scope and interval. A prompt may request a role, but does not establish Actor identity, Authority, Capability or successful execution.

**Actor vs. Role vs. Profile.** Actor is the stable participant; Role Binding is contextual responsibility; Capability/Adaptation Profiles describe a runtime's abilities and state. Several roles per actor and several actors per role are possible. A profile change need not change identity; a capability is not a permission.

**Outcome vs. Verification vs. Completion vs. Satisfaction.** Outcome describes a bounded Execution's terminal state; Verification assesses a claim against checks/Evidence; Completion assesses an assigned Contract; Satisfaction assesses an Expectation. A successful Outcome neither proves Verification nor automatically completes a Contract or satisfies its human objective.

**Thread vs. Conversation vs. runtime session.** Thread is a durable semantic/provenance boundary; Conversation groups exchange around one active Expectation; runtime session is transient provider/process context. None is automatically a substitute for another.

### 2.5 Extension Vocabulary — Temporal and Relational Terms

The following terms clarify **analysis and candidate contracts**; they do not create new X dimensions or canonical roots.

| Term | Intended meaning | Must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal role | Meaning of a time value for a particular referent | One universally meaningful `timestamp` |
| Temporal qualification | `(referent, role, value, context)` | Time entity or independent authority |
| Predicate type | Declared meaning of a relation between permitted referent kinds | A visual glyph or necessarily a DB enum |
| Qualified relation | Referents linked by a predicate with optional scope/time/evidence | Unqualified reference or automatically verified fact |
| Relation Type Contract | Signature and constraints of an allowed relation | Interface/schema Contract or the X Ξ Contract scope |
| Pattern | Reusable composition of qualified referents and relations | Canonical object or mandatory workflow |
| Protocol / method specification | Versioned description of how a procedure or assessment is performed | Proof that the procedure was performed |
| Decomposition | Navigate from pattern/view to its retained constituents and qualifiers | Predicate inversion or information recovery |
| Composition | Build qualified patterns from explicitly linked elements | Automatic semantic inference or proof |
| Derivation | Generate method-qualified representation or assertion | Mathematical differentiation or guaranteed reversibility |
| Projection | Select/aggregate qualified information for presentation/context | Complete reconstruction from a lossy output |
| Causal claim | Assertion that one occurrence contributed to or triggered another | Mere time order or correlation |

**Terminology hierarchy:** *Ontology* assigns a primary meaning/home; *Nomenclature* prevents collisions; *Relational Grammar* describes admissible predicates and composition; *Methodology* describes ways of applying such compositions; *Topology* decides actual technical bindings. Do not create a duplicate semantics for `Execution`, `Contract`, `Observation`, `Result` or `Outcome` through this extension.

**Retired from active vocabulary:** The historical spelling `Axion` has no assigned AIAEAO meaning. It remains identifiable **only as an unresolved historical source expression in the Research Residuum**; do not silently normalize it to `Axiom` or `Action`. Axiom is separately used in its established formal-logical sense (§2.7).

### 2.6 Extension Vocabulary — Measurement and Mathematical Methods

The terms below refine the **qualification of existing referents**, not the number of axes, X Scopes or Y/Z roots.

| Term | Meaning in this candidate | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Feature / attribute definition | Declared empirical/semantic meaning and permitted value domain of a property | A measured value, an assessment or a numeric feature vector by itself |
| Measurement instance | Value observed, declared or computed for a referent under specific conditions | Infallible fact or necessarily new persistent object |
| Nominal scale | Categories preserved by injective relabeling; equality/partition meaningful | Arithmetic on arbitrary category codes |
| Ordinal scale | Ordered classes preserved by strictly increasing transformations | Equal interval spacing or justified arithmetic mean |
| Interval scale | Differences meaningful under positive affine recoding (`x ↦ a·x + b`, `a > 0`) | Ratios of absolute scale values |
| Ratio scale | Meaningful zero and positive rescaling (`x ↦ a·x`, `a > 0`) | Automatic physical/economic additivity or unrestricted operations |
| Metric scale | Umbrella term for interval and ratio scales in the classical four-level model | A fifth independent level alongside ratio |
| Unit / dimension / quantity kind | Unit of expression, compatible physical/economic dimension, and meaning of the quantity | A scale level alone; currency and contextual conversions need separate rules |
| Value shape | Scalar, vector, series, distribution, histogram or other result form | The numeric scale of the elements |
| Measurement Scale Contract (M01) | Candidate feature-/measurement-qualification profile | New X dimension, Y root or automatically verified measurement |
| Function / Formula Contract (F01) | Candidate signature, preconditions, implementation/version and derivation limits | New canonical method object or automatic authorization |
| Formula Library | Versioned declarative specifications | Executed calculation or guaranteed correctness |
| Function Library / Registry | Discoverable implementations with contracts and tool bindings | Authority to invoke an effectful tool |
| Method / Protocol Library | Composition and policy for when/why functions are applied | A formula equivalent to a workflow or a mathematical theorem |

**Measurement versus evaluation.** A feature's empirical *property* and its assessed *quality* remain distinct. A numeric value, metric, score, criterion and epistemic Conclusion may all be linked, but one does not substitute for another. A numeric ordinal code is still ordinal unless a separately warranted quantitative embedding is declared. Qualifiers are not intrinsically trustworthy; the source of their declaration may require attestation.

### 2.7 Logic, Proof & Dialectics Vocabulary

This subsection provides **terminological grounding**, not new canonical root entities or an unclaimed theorem prover.

| Formal term | Established meaning / AIAEAO reading |
|---|---|
| Proposition | Statement considered for validity/truth under a declared language and interpretation |
| Premise | Assumption/input used by an inference; source assurance remains separate |
| Axiom | Starting statement accepted *within a named formal system* without derivation inside that system; not automatically empirically true |
| Inference rule | Versioned, explicitly admissible step from premises to conclusion |
| Lemma | Proven auxiliary proposition |
| Theorem | Proposition derivable under a specified formal calculus, axioms and proof rules |
| Corollary | Consequence of a prior proved statement under disclosed inference conditions |
| Conjecture / hypothesis | Unproved proposition / empirically testable claim; neither equals an Expectation |
| Proof / proof trace | Checkable derivation under named rules; premise authenticity and applicability still require separate assurance |
| Counterexample | Case refuting a general claim under the relevant assumptions |
| Thesis / antithesis | Positions in a dialectical procedure; antithesis need not be a logical negation |
| Opposition / contradiction | Contrast versus formal incompatibility **under the same relevant scope, interpretation and time** |
| Synthesis | Optional constructed position; does not imply Verification, theoremhood or resolution |

**Methodological distinctions:** Formal theoremhood ≠ synthetic test pass ≠ empirical verification ≠ internal consistency ≠ authorization. Opposition may be divergence, contradiction, comparability conflict or unresolved; synthesis is **never forced**. The exact contradiction calculus remains a research/method binding, not a new canonical logical operator.

### 2.9 Symbolic Grammar v0.9.3 — Representation, Not Identity

**Notation source:** `AIAEAO_VG_v0.9.3_Symbolic_Registry.json`, linked by content hash in the delivery manifest; its **129 single-codepoint visual primary anchors** represent **22 X, 63 Y, 31 Z and 13 UI/presentation records**. These counts are *symbol-register rows*, **not** 129 separate ontological objects, roots or runtime enums. Four historical IDs resolve in the lexicon as aliases, not second canonical definitions.

| Layer | Stable meaning | Representation and rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Semantic ID** | Stable machine-readable name, e.g. `contract-pairs.authority` | ASCII/structured identifier; never the Unicode glyph or a normalized font variant |
| **Primary visual anchor** | Exactly one globally unique **Unicode codepoint** in the VG register | Example Authority **⬭**, Admission **⬬**; not a permission or proof claim |
| **Derived notation** | Qualified, compositional expression | `Γᵖ`, `⟦EX⟧`, captions, boundary overlays and colored SVG projections are *not* alternate primary anchors |
| **Display qualification** | Readability: family, case, form, optional color | Never promotes truth, Authority, Admission, source trust, identity or ontology home |

**Quoted referents and bindability (D.3/D.5).** In the Compositional Grammar v0.2 draft, a source reference has a full `home:layer:concept["referent"]` address, for example `Y:α:Annotation["claim:A"]`. The quoted string is a referent locator under a declared resolution context, not the concept Semantic ID, glyph, proof of existence or grant of access. A valid binding requires an instantiable concept/Scope and a compatible identified referent. The five X dimensions Ψ/Γ/Σ/Ξ/Π are viewpoints, not instantiable referent types: `X:α:Σ["q"]` is invalid as a reference binding. Scope references such as `X:α:Event["event:e1"]` remain possible under their actual type/layer contract. This clarifies grammar rule G-REF-01 without mandating a parser, store or technical ID format.

**Letter classes.** X dimension labels use mathematical sans-serif bold Greek (**𝝭 𝝘 𝝨 𝝣 𝝥**); Y type/reference letter anchors use mathematical bold Fraktur (e.g. **𝕬 𝕽 𝕻 𝕾 𝕮 𝕴**); Z module type labels use mathematical sans-serif bold Latin (**𝗞 𝗖 𝗕 𝗧 𝗔 𝗜 𝗥 𝗣 𝗗**). Qualification coordinate display tokens use double-struck (**𝕋, 𝔽, 𝕄, 𝕆**). The canonical coordinate **names remain ASCII** `X,Y,Z,T,F,M,O`, not Unicode IDs. This display classification is not a claim that each family of Y/Z objects *must* be letter-shaped: states, arrows, zones and boundaries have their own atomic glyphs.

**Case convention:** X uppercase denotes a dimension, a lowercase style may denote a context-qualified *application*; Y/Z uppercase denotes a type/concept, lowercase may denote a *concretely identified and bound instance*. Lowercase never creates an instance, a state transition or an admission event merely by appearing in a view. T/F/M/O coordinate markers do not add four ontology homes.

**Operations and display:** The 26 registered formal mathematical operators, including `=`, `<`, `>`, `⊆`, `⊢` and `⊨`, are separate from AIAEAO concept pictograms; they require **no enclosing SVG shape or color**. A pictogram's mathematical provenance is not automatically its AIAEAO logical semantics. The six accepted pictogram overlaps, including the modallogical `⟡ ⟢ ⟣` and selected `⧊ ⧨ ⧩`, remain recorded as **display-only exceptions**, not formal proof or authorization operators. Form is generally intrinsic to the glyph; color is optional, nonsemantic and never an authority signal.

**Unicode normalization caveat:** Mathematical styled letters may fold to ordinary letters under compatibility normalization (NFKC). Do **not** derive object IDs, lookups, event fields, trust or hash identity from glyph codepoints or their rendered appearance. Font availability and glyph-backed SVG previews are not proof of font-independent vector fallback.

**Taxonomic multi-placement:** Authority has one X definition and the primary anchor `contract-pairs.authority` **⬭**. A Governance/Boundary view in Z may reference it through historical `governance-boundary.authority`, but does not define another Authority or primary glyph. Admission follows the same rule (**⬬**). The historic `contract-pairs.outcome` ID is a **version-scoped navigation/rename alias** for `contract-pairs.conclusion` (**⭓**); this is *not* semantic equivalence with Y `execution-objects.outcome` (**𝕺**, visual candidate pending aesthetic approval). `contract-pairs.preception` remains a spelling alias of `contract-pairs.perception` (**⭔**). A data migration must consult the origin version and historical meaning before resolving any such alias.

The full per-ID glyph assignments, four aliases, 15 context-dependent review flags, and mathematical-meaning warnings are contained in the associated register and indexed in **Annex J**; the source of truth for the visual mapping is the separately versioned VG registry, not a new Morphology root inventory.

## 3. Morphology

Morphology establishes the **complete classification and navigation frame**. It identifies primary homes and qualified questions; it does not itself authorize an action, evaluate a claim or prescribe physical implementation. Each canonical element retains exactly one X/Y/Z ontological home (§6). T/F/M/O extend description and navigation **without adding roots**. Cross-axis connections remain explicit relations.

In the VG the default **presentation order** is `X/Y/Z + F/M/T/O`; ordering the visible tabs never changes the registered coordinate vocabulary or the α/β/ζ-binding semantics. They need not be numerically valued, mutually independent or present for every referent. Extra analytical properties (scale, trust, information loss, sorting, uncertainty) must be expressed through qualifications/relations/methods, **not additional main coordinates**. The ontology primary-home count remains three, not seven.

The **Identified / Resource Objects** family includes the seven resource-chain objects already present in Visual Grammar and the canonical work, execution and interpretive objects previously placed under X for historical reasons. This is a **classification repair**, not an assertion that an Outcome is a file or that an Annotation must have an independent storage record. An object may be a bounded record, durable object, referenced state or attached interpretation; §6 states which.

X states (e.g. action lifecycle) and Y resource states are **values**, not additional axes. Skeleton forms and colors are renderings (§4), not canonical model elements. The Unicode symbols are Visual Grammar anchors, not schema fields.

### 3.2 Ordering per Axis

Each axis has its own ordering principle: X **logical**, Y **identity-to-state / resource derivation**, Z **inner-to-outer / boundary reach**. Ordering is a reading discipline, not an implied total order for runtime execution.

#### X — logical ordering

1. Dimensions (v9.3 VG anchors): **𝝭 Epistemology → 𝝘 Governance → 𝝨 Semantics → 𝝣 Execution → 𝝥 Provenance**. The plain Greek letters Ψ/Γ/Σ/Ξ/Π remain semantic notation in mathematical and historical contexts.
2. Within each dimension: **Scope → first Key Pair endpoint → second Key Pair endpoint → relevant relationships**. The full Logic Relations family is then described explicitly rather than hidden under an arbitrary dimension.
3. Logic Relations, from rule to judgment: invariant ⟡ → constraint ⬙ → compatibility ⬘ → authorization ⬗ → verification ⟢ / refutation ⟣ (AIAEAO pictograms; not the reserved formal mathematical operators). Verification and refutation are alternatives, not rankings.

#### Y — identity-to-state ordering

1. Identity Anchors: **Actor 𝕬 → Role Binding 𝕽 → Principal 𝕻 → Session Binding 𝕾 → Capability Profile 𝕮 → Adaptation Profile 𝕴**.
2. Resource chain: **Source ⍠ → Resource ⌹ → Content ⌻ → Revision ⍰ → Artifact ⌺ → Evidence ⌼ → Cache ⍞** (Cache is derived, never the authority source).
3. Additional identified objects: **Work Item → bounded Execution → Outcome (its terminal state) → Annotation (interpretation of a referenced object)**. Their order is explanatory, not a claim that each depends on the preceding one. Semantic Classification is a form/value of Annotation, **not an additional root object**.
4. Relation Arrows, in partial process/reading order: selection ↣ → reference → / uses-specification ⇢ → admits ↪ / refuses ↛ → performed-by ↠ → executed-as ⇛ → production ⇥ → observation ↝ → causation ⇰ → derivation ↦ → projection ⇉.

#### Z — materialization and boundary ordering

1. Module Bodies: **Core → Connector → Toolbox → Tool → Adapter → Integration → Runtime → Provider → Third-party Dependency**.
2. Governance & Boundary (widest to narrowest as a reading convention): **Boundary ⬠ → Project Boundary ⬕ → Thread Boundary ⬖ → Grant ⧨ → Refusal ⧩ → Capability ⬒ → Capability Boundary ⬓ → Execution Boundary ⬔ → Visibility ⧊**. **X cross-references:** Policy ⬥, Authority ⬭, Admission ⬬. This is neither a total boundary nesting nor a promotion of X concepts into Z..
3. Resource Zones (material to projected): **Ownership ⬦ → Resource Zone ⬧ → Back Office ◾ → Front Office ◻ → Front Stage ◽**; Zone Boundary ⬪ and Same Resource Across Zones ⬨ are cross-references.

This Z ordering does **not** assert that UI zones are physical nested containers or that all the listed boundaries have the same ontology. Module, governance and presentation location are different kinds of spatial reference, united only by their question of *where / within what limit*.

### 3.5 Complete Primary-Home Rule

The three-axis frame is a **complete primary-home classification** of canonical concepts in this document; this assertion concerns *where defined concepts are assigned*, not an exhaustive inventory of all possible future features or measurements. The following placement principles apply:

- **X** owns the five dimensions, their Scope/Key Pair definitions and their logic relations. Its section may reference work, evidence, execution records or annotations but must **not redefine them**.
- **Y** owns identity anchors, the resource chain, Work Item, bounded Execution, Outcome and Annotation. Semantic Classification is a referenced Annotation classification, not a sixth X primitive.
- **Z** owns structural/module placement, Project and Thread boundaries, execution limits and the resource/presentation zone vocabulary. It refers back to X when Policy, Authority and Admission appear as boundary aspects.
- A reference may belong to several dimensions analytically, but its **definition remains at one address**. A qualified status or projection does not automatically create a new canonical entity.
- The classification is **conceptual**. Decisions about tables, stores, record kinds, serialization and schema fields belong to §7.

### 3.6 Temporal Qualification — T (WHEN)

**T is a temporal qualification over X/Y/Z referents, not a fourth primary ontology home.** The canonical definition homes remain X/Y/Z. A timestamp gains meaning only when its **referent** and **temporal role** are known. Consequently, `timestamp` by itself must not be assumed to mean occurrence, ingestion, observation, evaluation or validity time interchangeably.

| Temporal facet | Question | Existing referents | Qualification to examine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occurrence | When did it happen? | Event, Action, Result | `occurred_at` / event clock provenance |
| Recording / admission | When was it admitted into canonical history? | Event, Core ingestion | `recorded_at`, ordering / sequence |
| Observation | When and by whom was it perceived? | Observation, Perception, Actor | `observed_at` and observation context |
| Assessment | When was a judgment reached? | Conclusion, Satisfaction, Completion, Annotation | `assessed_at`, applicable method revision |
| Effective interval | When did a binding or rule apply? | Role Binding, Grant, Policy, Session Binding | `effective_from` / `effective_until` or interval |
| Freshness / expiry | Until when is a derived representation reusable? | Cache, Revision, Resource | source-version fingerprint, `valid_until`, invalidation condition |
| Historicity | What was available or known at a past point? | Thread, Revision, Evidence, Context | `as_of` reconstruction / knowledge availability |
| Scheduling / dependency | What must precede or constrain an action? | Work Item, Execution, Event | deadlines, time guards and partial order |

**Readable role tokens (D.1).** The eight existing facets may be named `occurrence`, `recording`, `observation`, `assessment`, `effective-interval`, `freshness`, `historicity` and `scheduling` in an explicitly selected notation/profile. This is a lexical correspondence, **not** a frozen field list, runtime enum, clock conversion or migration of existing timestamps.

**Order is not scheduling (D.2).** A partial order of distributed Events can describe occurrence/observation relationships without any plan, deadline or execution instruction. Keep its role and declared relation/order basis explicit; chronology or a logical clock alone does not establish causation. No ninth temporal role is introduced. The earlier Grammar v0.2 distributed example's `role="scheduling", order="partial"` remains a historical experimental spelling; it must not be inferred to request scheduling. The aligned example uses `role="occurrence", order="partial"` under an explicitly identified logical-clock profile. Interpretation and comparability still require that profile.

The column of suggested qualifications is **not a frozen field list**. An implementation may encode the same semantics through a referenced record, typed property or qualified relation. Persistent storage and ephemeral runtime context must be distinguished without introducing a pseudo-coordinate or storage shorthand. Persisted ≠ canonical; runtime ≠ ephemeral in every provider; durable bytes ≠ promoted project authority. The decisive boundaries remain Project/Thread, admitted ingestion, and explicit promotion (§§5.4, 6.4.2, 7.5).

**Historical-state rule.** Distinguish (a) what occurred, (b) what was recorded by a given time, (c) what a specific Actor could access, (d) what was actually supplied or perceived, and (e) what a later observer concludes. A reconstruction must mark missing external state, non-replayed provider context and unavailable evidence rather than manufacture historical certainty.

### 3.7 Functional Composition — F (HOW)

**F describes methods or transformations over qualified referents; it is not a new canonical ontology home.** In a useful shorthand, `f(x, y, z, t; θ) → observations / selections / effects`, where `θ` represents method and variant parameters. This shorthand is not a mathematical assertion that every output is deterministic, differentiable or invertible.

An Actor or provider applying a method is identified through **Y** and bounded through **Z**; the method itself may span X judgments, Y objects, Z boundaries and T qualifications. Thus F is associated with *who acts*, but **not contained exclusively under Y**. A cache invalidation rule, for example, can be specified without asserting the presence of a human agent.

Candidate method families: **decision**, **dialectics**, **experimentation**, **exploration**, **evaluation**, **feedback/regulation**, **projection**, **promotion**. These name possible compositions, **not** new canonical objects, sixth X dimensions or guaranteed shipped capabilities. Their source elements retain primary homes (§§5.6–5.9, 6.5, 7.18).

### 3.8 Navigation, Scale and Order

This reference distinguishes four simultaneous organizational questions:

| Kind | Governs | Existing or proposed rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition order** | Where is a term primarily defined? | Exactly one X/Y/Z ontology home (§§3.5, 6) |
| **Logical order** | Which constraints or dependencies matter? | X nesting and explicit typed relations; never automatically a total timeline |
| **Temporal order** | What happened / was known / was valid first? | Event timestamp, sequence, causation and time qualifications; partial orders supported |
| **Presentation order** | In what sequence is a view displayed? | Derived projection / user navigation; may vary without redefining canon |

The current X reading order remains Ψ → Γ → Σ → Ξ → Π; Y and Z retain §3.2 ordering. A user-selected ordering or zoom level may alter a **projection**, never silently reassign ontological homes. No generic persistent user-ordering contract is accepted in this candidate.

**Composition / Decomposition terminology.** *Composition* builds a qualified pattern from referenced constituents; *Decomposition* traverses its **documented** constituents and relations. Bidirectional navigation is not inverse semantics of a directed predicate. Separately, *Derivation* computes a new assertion/representation under a method, and *Projection/Aggregation* may selectively discard information. Structural traceability does not imply informational reversibility. See §5.8.

### 3.9 Measurement and Formula Reach Across X/Y/Z/T/F

**MEAS-01 / FUNC-01 (legacy M01/F01) are profiles, not extra coordinates.** A feature definition can be a versioned Y Artifact/Revision specification; a measurement concerns an existing Y Resource, Execution, Evidence or other properly referenced subject; its meaning is classified and assessed through the relevant X Dimension/Scope; constraints and execution belong to Z Boundaries/Toolbox/Tool; T qualifies validity and relevant revisions; R01 connects the input and derivation provenance; F describes permitted composition. A single feature may appear in multiple views without being redefined in several primary homes.

| Concern | Questions to preserve | Example without new roots |
|---|---|---|
| Scale / transformation | Which re-encodings preserve meaning? | Ordinal priority is invariant under strictly increasing relabeling |
| Units and dimensionality | Can these quantities be compared, added or converted? | Milliseconds vs seconds; EUR vs duration; rate vs stock |
| Quantity kind / additivity | Does the domain permit sum or ratio? | Kelvin temperature is ratio-scaled but an absolute-temperature sum is not licensed merely by its scale |
| Shape / missingness | What is the cardinality and data form? | Histogram of category frequencies is not a scalar |
| Time and provenance | When, how and on whose evidence was it obtained? | Expired observation is not reusable; missing source assurance stays visible |
| Method signature / invocation | Which formula and which particular implementation were used? | Protocol Artifact Revision linked to bounded Execution |

**No automatic morpho-to-feature-space mapping.** Morphological X/Y/Z coordinates are conceptual classification axes, not a metric vector in machine-learning space. An encoder or foreign mapping must explicitly define any such transformation, its units and its information loss.

### 3.10 Mode (M) and Observer Perspective (O)

**M — Mode.** A mode qualifies the operational/interaction regime under which a qualified method, request or projection is considered (e.g., illustrative `simulation` versus `operative`). A declared mode changes neither a referent's primary home nor its Authority. The actual list of mode values, state transitions and binding to existing Stage/MODE usage are **not frozen**. Do not identify Mode with method, lifecycle stage, Outcome or grant.


**v9.3 VG-only interpretive reading:** `Loose` (explorative hypotheses, visibly provisional), `Transitional` (typed, scoped relation with explicit gaps and assumptions) and `Strict` (claim must exhibit the required proof/admission/witness prerequisites, otherwise unresolved/refused as appropriate). These are **three optional visual/interpretive labels**, neither a normative three-valued `MODE` enum nor a temporal Stage sequence. They do **not** loosen technical authority gates in `Loose`, do not grant rights in `Transitional`, and do not automatically establish truth in `Strict`. Runtime values, transitions and persistence contracts remain open in the Research Residuum. The prior illustrative `simulation` / `operative` distinction remains an example of a different possible operational classification; no forced equivalence is adopted.

**O — Observer Perspective.** An observer perspective qualifies who may access something, which view/time/scope is selected, what context was actually **delivered**, and what the actor is later *claimed to have perceived*. An Actor belongs to Y; O does not establish a second Actor identity. O also does not duplicate the **Ψ Observation** X-Scope, whose endpoints remain Perception ↔ Conclusion. `available` ≠ `permitted` ≠ `delivered` ≠ `perceived` ≠ `concluded`.

**Cross-coordinate example:** One Event (Π/X), performed by Actor A (Y), admitted in Thread T (Z), occurred at `t1` and was recorded at `t2` (T), evaluated by a versioned protocol (F) in simulation mode (M), may be available in an observer-restricted context (O). None of these references implies that the observer actually read the Event or that the simulated Action occurred externally.

### 3.11 Closed Coordinate Set and Open Research

**Axis closure is not knowledge closure.** The **seven coordinate names are fixed for v9.1's model-navigation grammar**; research may still refine predicates, qualifiers, profiles, specific method contracts and technical schemas. Changing that inventory would require an explicit future model revision, never the incidental introduction of a term.

For a **carefully scoped universe of admissible propositions** `U` partitioned into accepted/consolidated claims `A` and unresolved claims `R`, one may use the conceptual mnemonic `A = U \ R` (set difference, not arithmetic subtraction). **The subtraction is of qualified claims, not ontology entities**; refuted, out-of-scope and historical assertions must not be silently included in `A` by treating all non-open material as accepted. `O` remains reserved for Observer Perspective, not the universe of claims. The Research Residuum retains the open claims and their proof/decision gates; a conceptual review decision does not satisfy an empirical or runtime gate.

### 3.12 Layered Coordinate Binding — α / β / ζ (tested hypothesis, not new ontology)

The three names distinguish **description / technical binding / admitted historical instantiation**. They are **not** extra navigation axes or separate ontology roots. In this **candidate binding grammar**, `M` belongs to **every** layer (not only to Thread). All five coordinates `(X,Y,Z,F,M)` remain present; `O` and `T` progressively cease to be *free analytical variables*, not necessarily to exist as information.

```text
α  MORPHOLOGY        (X, Y, Z, F, M, T, O)  [7 free coordinates]
           ↓ Bind_O : observer/visibility/delivery rules, qualified references
β  TOPOLOGY          (X, Y, Z, F, M, T)     [6 free coordinates]
           ↓ Bind_T : occurrence/recording/validity/as-of witnesses
ζ  CANONICAL THREAD  (X, Y, Z, F, M)        [5 free coordinates]
```

Let `K = {X,Y,Z,F,M}`, `α = K ∪ {T,O}`, `β = K ∪ {T}`, `ζ = K`. Therefore `ζ ⊂ β ⊂ α` **as sets of coordinate names by definition**; this is a tautological set fact, **not** the experimental result. What requires evidence is a meaningful **conformance mapping** between the layers, including preserved referents, predicates, boundaries, Mode and resolvable bindings of O and T. The arrows signify **constrained specialization** (`≈ Bind_O`, `≈ Bind_T`), not bijective functions, information erasure, automatic Event admission, or a forced runtime sequence.

| Layer | Scope of the signature | What a conforming mapping must retain | What it must not infer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **α / Morphology** | Definitions, admissible relations, methods, modes and possible observer/time qualifications | X/Y/Z primary homes; qualified T/O semantics; epistemic/governance negative space | an ontology definition is itself an implemented tool or an accepted historical Event |
| **β / Topology** | Applicable bindings/contracts, technical rules and revalidation paths | observer/visibility rules and their revision/evidence; temporal roles still free; authority/effect checks | a technical mapping proves actual delivery/perception or fixes one universal Observer |
| **ζ / Canonical Thread** | admitted historical referents, actions/results, boundaries, method and concrete Mode | persisted or resolvable time witnesses, `as_of` availability, observer-relative reconstructability when authorized | T/O have been deleted; a fingerprint proves freshness; recorded=occurred; delivered=perceived |

**Normative reference constraint (conditional):** For any implementation claiming this layered mapping, preservation of meaning is more important than dimensional counts. `X/Y/Z/F/M` must not be retyped across transitions; loss of required Observer/Time evidence must be visible as *unknown/unavailable* or cause a fail-closed action, not fabricated reconstruction. A canonical Event remains a historical occurrence; multiple views do not produce duplicate canonical Events.

**Evidence and falsifiability:** `EXP-015-NCI` constructed 120/120 valid finite synthetic mappings with an author-built flat control; 15/15 authored mutations were detected. The *strong erasure hypothesis* (T/O information can disappear entirely) fails on distinct Observer visibility and `occurred_at` vs `recorded_at`/`as_of` cases. No experiment proves necessity, minimality, unique expressive power, arbitrary recursion depth, or Core/Adapter conformance. See §§4.10, 7.37, 7.38 and `RES-LAYER-01` in the Research Residuum.

## 4. Phenomenology

Phenomenology describes **the angle of observation and what becomes perceptible from it**: the approach to possible understanding, not a claim that a view itself constitutes knowledge. Morphology supplies the space; Phenomenology supplies the standpoint; Methodology (§5) connects and evaluates the X pairs; Epistemology (§6.2.1) states what evidence justifies a Conclusion.

### 4.1 Perspective, Appearance and Access to Justified Understanding

An observer never necessarily sees the entire canonical Thread. What appears depends on actor, purpose, scope, permissions, visibility, selected granularity, time and source limitations. These are **filters and presentations of one model**, not alternative truths.

```text
Canonical admitted records + references (technical binding in §7)
                │
                ├── permitted + visible subset
                ├── observer perspective / purpose / scale
                ├── selected projection or Context Assembly
                ▼
      PERCEIVABLE APPEARANCE (phenomenological access)
                │
                ▼
      PERCEPTION → assessment → CONCLUSION (Ψ, §§5–6)
```

**Observation ≠ justified understanding.** An Observation provides the X-Scope for Perception and Conclusion; warranted understanding depends on the qualified Conclusion and underlying Evidence. No additional epistemic endpoint is introduced. Viewing a projection, logging an Event, collecting a Result or reading a model response does not itself produce justified knowledge.

### 4.2 Observer, Scope and Visibility

**Authorization** determines what an Actor may access or do (Γ; see §6.2.2 and §6.4.2). **Visibility** determines what is surfaced in a particular view. A permitted resource can still be muted; a visible item is not necessarily executable. The execution boundary constrains effects independently of the view.

The observer may be human, AI agent, service or review process. Changes of observer or scale do not change canonical identity. The same Resource can be shown in several zones (§6.4.3) with differing detail and salience, subject to permission and provenance preservation.

### 4.3 RAW Thread and Derived Appearances

The RAW Thread is canonical and loss-aware; the following are **derived, potentially selective** appearances:

```text
RAW THREAD
    ├── Planning View
    ├── Technical View
    ├── Topic View
    ├── Conversation View
    ├── Execution View
    ├── Rights / Visibility View
    ├── Goal / Satisfaction View
    ├── CTO / CFO / Yield View
    ├── Summary
    ├── Retrieval / RAG
    ├── Compression
    ├── Consolidated Knowledge
    └── Memory
```

**Compression may reduce representation but must not destroy provenance.** An aggregate, score, summary, retrieved excerpt or memory can support review, but does not substitute for admitted canonical facts. Every derived appearance should retain enough source linkage to disclose omissions, uncertainty and the difference between supplied facts and inferences.

### 4.4 Context Is Not Thread State

The complete Thread and the context delivered to an Actor are distinct. Strategies may include: last message only; n−1 context; summary plus recent turns; relevant execution results; retrieved foreign Thread; rights-filtered and visibility-filtered context. Such choices belong to **Context Assembly**, not Thread persistence. Capability and Adaptation Profiles (§6.3.1) can inform strategy without granting Authority.

**Boundary rule (v9.1):** Context Assembly is a purpose- and actor-directed **selection/transformation operation**, not merely Retrieval or Summary and not a second canonical truth store. It must distinguish *eligible*, *permitted*, *selected*, *delivered*, *actually perceived* and *retrospectively inferred* material. A provider's hidden state is not assumed reconstructable; see §4.9 and §5.15.

### 4.5 Visual Grammar and Resource Zones

Visual Grammar is a **projection grammar**, not canonical data. It should be able to render every canonical object and relationship with stable distinctions: timeline/replay; planning and technical views; actor/role views; permission/visibility views; contract views; cost/yield lenses; multidimensional exploration.

The Z-axis names Front Stage, Front Office and Back Office as **zones of appearance/operational placement** (§6.4.3). Phenomenology explains how they are perceived; their primary Z definitions remain in Ontology. The reading rule *form first, symbol second, colour optional* may help rendering but never alters canonical semantics.

If Visual Grammar conflicts with accepted ontology/technical bindings, it must be adjusted. UI geometry and rendering state do not enter canonical Thread records merely because they are visible.

___

### 4.6 Time-dependent Observation and Historical Appearance

An observer-relative view is also time-relative. `visible now`, `permitted then`, `present in the historical Thread`, `available to a particular Actor`, and `actually supplied in Context Assembly` are different claims. A projection must retain the time qualification it used and must not conflate later annotations or current policy with a historical point of view.

For historical replay or a retrospective assessment, the view should disclose: chosen reference time; relevant recording/occurrence-time semantics; source revision where available; authorization and visibility basis; omitted or externally opaque state; and whether a judgment is **contemporaneous** or **retrospective**. A snapshot or derived Memory is not evidence that an Actor originally perceived all its contents.

### 4.7 Composition / Decomposition User Navigation

A *decomposition view* exposes a chosen pattern's **documented** constituent elements, primary ontology homes, incoming/outgoing typed assertions, qualifiers and provenance. A *composition view* presents a justified or explicitly limited higher-order pattern with drill-down links. Such links do not invert predicates and cannot recreate discarded or opaque source information. A user may enter either direction: from **Conclusion → Evidence / method / time** or from **Experiment → trials → individual Actions and Results**.

A projection may aggregate or reorder. It must not imply that every logical relation was executed, that an inferred relation was observed, or that a composite pattern automatically establishes Verification, Authority, Completion or Satisfaction. Visual Grammar retains no authority to redefine relation meaning.

### 4.8 Quantitative Appearances and Loss-Conscious Projections

A UI may display category codes, numeric measurements, ranks, grades, confidence estimates, temporal validity and formula outputs using the same charting primitives. **Shared presentation does not establish commensurability.** A derived graph must retain or disclose the scale definition, units, precision and value shape relevant to interpretation. A change of bins, normalization, currency convention, feature encoder or assessment criteria may change appearance without changing the underlying Source/Resource identity.

**Display invariant (proposed):** projections must not silently transform ordinal codes into metric distances, interval origins into ratio zeros, or a sample of observations into claims of exhaustive coverage. Source revisions, method revisions and sampling/visibility limits should remain traceable where material. Presentation order and plotted proximity never imply causality, authorization or equivalence.

### 4.9 Context Assembly and Projection — Distinct Derived Operations

**Context Assembly** creates a bounded context for a specified target Actor, purpose, time/as-of basis and operation, subject to Authority/Visibility, source revision, context-budget and information-loss requirements. **Projection** creates a view/representation for an observer; it may use common selection machinery but is not necessarily the same delivered runtime input.

| Concern | Context Assembly | Projection |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Material delivered to Actor/runtime for a task | Material surfaced for perception/navigation/report |
| Required qualification | target, purpose, permissions, time, selected references, method and limits | observer, permission, perspective, ordering, omission, method and limits |
| Canonical effect | None merely by assembling | None merely by viewing |
| When to retain descriptor | When delivery was significant to an Execution/Conclusion/Selection | When the view materially underpinned a Conclusion/Selection/Execution |
| Evidential limit | Delivered context ≠ actual perception or model's hidden state | Visible item ≠ authorized effect or justified Conclusion |

**Persistence threshold:** Pure cosmetic sorting/layout may remain ephemeral. A decision-relevant Assembly/Projection should leave a stable, resolvable **descriptor, source/revision links, selection rules and omission/coverage statements**, without automatically persisting a new canonical root. A descriptor fingerprint is neither proof of delivery nor proof of freshness; its usage and admission evidence are separate.

**Ordering:** A `presentation.ordering_spec` can designate `sort_by`, direction, tie-breaking and grouping for a derived view. Definition order, logical dependency order and historical partial order remain distinct. Display position never implies causality or evidence ranking.

### 4.10 Observer and As-of Reconstruction Across α / β / ζ

Under §3.12, **O is bound at α→β** as an explicit contract/rule context, not resolved once for every future observer; **T is bound at β→ζ** by declared temporal roles, not deleted from historically queried records. A `ζ` record may therefore be viewed by several observers and at several `as_of` cutoffs; those are **derived appearances of one historically identified referent** (§§4.2–4.9).

A successful reference projection must answer separately: *is the record canonically admitted by the cutoff? can this Actor currently access it? was the Context delivered? is perception merely claimed/unknown?* The recipient's actual psychological perception is not a function of an Event reference or Context Fingerprint. `EXP-015-NCI` supplies finite counterexamples; **real delivery/audit and revocation traces remain untested** (`RES-UX-01`, `RES-TIME-01`, `RES-LAYER-01`).

### 4.11 Qualified Projection and Visual Evidence (v9.3)

The Reflector exposes one shared reference set through **REL, T as-of, O Observer/Context, F Method/Measurement and a graph projection**, supplemented by α/β/ζ binding and counterexample cases. These are **read-only or fixture-bounded illustrations**, not evidence of a deployed Core, Admission service, honest Provider state or actual delivery to a human observer.

A view may resolve a current claim, a historical recorded claim, or an explicitly unresolved claim under `as_of` and observer scope. The following separations are non-negotiable:

semantic_ID != Unicode_anchor != SVG_rendering
available != permitted != delivered != perceived != concluded
Result != independently verified Conclusion
shown in a graph != stored technical relationship != admitted Event
same projected item != second canonical entity

## 5. Methodology

Methodology concerns **the X-axis logic**: how the five Scope/Key Pairs can be traversed, composed and evaluated. It does not introduce a sixth dimension, a compulsory workflow engine or a technical container tree. The terms are formally defined in §6.2; this chapter explains their **connections**.

### 5.1 Five Key Pairs

| Dimension | Scope | Opening / input | Complement / judgment | Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ψ Epistemology | Observation | Perception | Conclusion | What is perceivable; what is justified? |
| Γ Governance | Policy | Authority | Admission | What is allowed; was the specific request admitted? |
| Σ Semantics | Conversation | Expectation | Satisfaction | What is intended; how well was it met? |
| Ξ Execution | Contract | Delegation | Completion | What responsibility was assigned; was it fulfilled? |
| Π Provenance | Event | Action | Result | What effect was attempted; what occurred? |

The five pairs are **related but not interchangeable**. An Admission is an authorization decision, not a guaranteed runtime success; a Result is not a terminal Outcome; a Conclusion is not Satisfaction. Policy and Observation may surround a run logically without creating artificial end-state objects.

### 5.2 Logical Nesting — Together

The conceptual arrangement follows the **nested scope structure** rather than the former linear chain. An opening side gives a frame; the complementary side evaluates or records its relevant closure. The words *scope end* in the diagram are visual delimiters, **not additional canonical states**.

```text
OBSERVATION (Ψ)
│
├── PERCEPTION                          available observations and limits
│   └── POLICY (Γ)                      applicable permission frame
│       ├── AUTHORITY                   source + scope of allowed actions
│       └── ADMISSION                   request admitted / refused
│           └── CONVERSATION (Σ)        admitted semantic activity
│               ├── EXPECTATION         intended result
│               │   └── CONTRACT (Ξ)    responsibility for that intention
│               │       ├── DELEGATION  assignment / acceptance conditions
│               │       │   └── EVENT (Π)
│               │       │       ├── ACTION
│               │       │       └── RESULT(s)
│               │       └── COMPLETION  assessment of assignment fulfillment
│               └── SATISFACTION        assessment of the Expectation
│       [POLICY applicability can continue or end; not a new state]
│
└── CONCLUSION                          justified assessment of what appeared
```

**Reading corrections and boundary cases:**

- **Authority precedes Admission conceptually**, but Admission is the operative gate: if refused, the action does not become an authorized execution. A denied attempt may itself be recorded via an authorized ingestion path; this does not mean the denied effect occurred.
- **Policy may be checked again during runtime** (revalidation, changed resources or scope). Such repeated checks are temporal behavior, not extra nesting layers in the abstract method.
- **One Contract may involve multiple Executions, Actions, Results or branches** as permitted by the implementation. The diagram does not force one-to-one cardinalities, technical child records or synchronous completion.
- **Outcome** is the terminal state of a **bounded Execution** (§6.3.2), based on relevant operational evidence. It is a *side reference* for deciding Completion, not an additional X Key Pair endpoint and not automatic proof of fulfillment.
- **Verification/refutation** qualify a claim or Conclusion through the X Logic Relations. They are neither an implicit consequence of Outcome nor an automatic promotion to canonical truth.
- **Satisfaction** may be assessed from results, outcomes, human judgment and verified support; it is not the same as Completion. A new Observation may begin after any Conclusion.

Consequently, **logical nesting ≠ technical containment ≠ timeline/replay**. §7 specifies record bindings; §4 presents observer-relative runtime appearances.

### 5.3 Evaluation Across the Five Dimensions

| Dimension | Primary question | Retained material / cross-reference |
|---|---|---|
| Epistemology | Which statements are justified and under what limits? | Perceptions, Conclusions, claims, limitations, Evidence links, refutation and verification status |
| Governance | Who may act, within which scope and constraints? | Policy, Authority, Admission/Refusal; Visibility and Execution Boundary as Z references |
| Semantics | What objective is being understood or pursued? | Conversation, Expectation, Satisfaction; Work Item as Y reference |
| Execution | What is assigned and fulfilled? | Contract, Delegation, Completion; bounded Execution and Outcome as Y references |
| Provenance | Who/what produced which recorded effect, when and from where? | Event, Action, Result; Actor, Annotation, Artifact/Evidence as Y references |

A practical method, without imposing a fixed runtime sequence, is to establish the objective and exclusions; separate declared facts, hypotheses and unknowns; bind Actors and permission scopes; perform admitted work; retain Actions, Results and evidence references; evaluate claims distinctly; decide Completion separately from Satisfaction; explicitly promote durable material when authorized; and revise or supersede append-orientedly.

### 5.4 Promotion: Runtime, Relay and Canonical Thread

The distinction **Relay Thread ≠ Canonical Thread** has its primary boundary definition in §6.4.2. Here it is treated as a **methodological promotion discipline**, not a second epistemic scope:

```text
runtime conversation  → transient reasoning context
Relay Thread          → persisted coordination context
review candidate      → tracked proposal
accepted artifact     → durable project authority in its declared scope
```

No arrow means automatic promotion. Each transition requires a suitable source, explicit review, authority and epistemic qualification. A Relay can transport observations and coordination context; it does not itself guarantee epistemic justification, Verification or accepted project truth.

|---|---|

| Γ Governance | The human authorizes preparation of a local candidate, not publication, deployment or automatic adoption. |
| Σ Semantics | The goal is to restore X/Y/Z consistency, reposition Phenomenology and separate technical definitions; unrelated implementation changes are out of scope. |
| Ξ Execution | Preparing the new document is bounded work. Producing a file is an Outcome-related fact, not automatic Contract Completion under all acceptance conditions. |

The conceptual objective does not authorize migration of legacy Threads, rewriting unrelated schemas or treating generated claims as accepted canonical project decisions.

### 5.6 Relational Grammar — Scope and Layer Separation

The model already has **three deliberately non-identical relation vocabularies**:

1. **X Logic Relations** (`⊤`, `⊴`, `⊪`, `⊩`, `⊨`, `⊭`) express rules or judgments (§6.2.6).
2. **Y Relation Arrows** express semantic connections, including Selection, Observation, Production, Causation and Derivation (§6.3.3).
3. **Technical Relationships** and named reference slots bind selected meanings in admitted records (§§7.4, 7.6).

These must **not** be flattened into one unrestricted list of predicates. A single *typing framework* can describe their signatures and composition requirements while leaving their different logical, semantic and storage responsibilities intact.

An abstract **qualified relation expression** is:

```text
Rel(subject_ref, predicate_type, object_ref; qualifiers)
qualifiers = {scope?, temporal?, origin?, evidence?, method?, status?, limitations?}
```

This is a **conceptual expression**, not a mandated persisted `Relation` root. Some expressions can be carried by direct `*_ref` fields; others may require a versioned relationship assertion linked to Event / Annotation / Evidence. `origin: inferred` is not equivalent to verified truth. A technical event that stores an assertion is not automatically proof of the asserted relationship.

### 5.7 Candidate Primitive Families and Composition Patterns

The following are **candidate analytical primitives** rather than a proven minimal algebra. Logical arity follows meaning; the five canonical X Key Pairs do **not** require all other domains to use pairs.

| Family | Abstract form | Existing evidence / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | `A != B` | Strong nomenclature non-equivalences; not an ordinary directed edge |
| Scoped complementarity | `Scope: A ↔ B` | Exactly five X Key Pairs; pair relation does not itself impose runtime sequence |
| Binding | `A —[role/context]→ B` | Y anchors and environment/resource references |
| Gate / constraint | `request + applicable rule → admission/refusal` | X Γ with Z scope; documenting a gate is not granting Authority |
| Qualification / evaluation | `evidence + criteria → assessment` | Ψ Conclusion and Σ Satisfaction must remain distinct |
| Derivation / causation | `derivedFrom` versus `causedBy` | Related but non-equivalent; chronology is not causality |
| Transition / supersession | `state/version₁ → state/version₂` | Revision, append-only, `supersedes`; not destructive overwrite |

Composite patterns can employ several primitives without introducing new ontology homes:

| Pattern | Components / indicative shape | Special constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision** | Judgment → Selection → (Admission) → Action | Conclusion ≠ selection ≠ authorized act; refusal may halt the sequence |
| **Dialectics** | Thesis ↔ opposing position → possible Synthesis | Opposition may remain unresolved; synthesis is not automatic Verification |
| **Experimentation** | Hypothesis + versioned Protocol → Trial(s) → Measurement → Assessment | Hold control/variant/inputs/environment and evaluation criteria distinguishable |
| **Feedback / Regulation** | Observation → Evaluation → authorized Adjustment → new Observation | Assessment alone does not authorize a state change |
| **Projection** | Canonical referent + visibility/context rules → derived Appearance | Selection and compression do not create new canon |
| **Promotion** | Candidate + Evidence + review + Authority → admitted reference/change | Stored or generated material never becomes accepted merely by persistence |

**Terminology limits.** *Hypothesis* is a testable claim, whereas *Expectation* specifies an intended result. *Thesis–Antithesis–Synthesis* is a heuristic dialectical pattern, not a universal logical theorem or an attribution to every dialectical school. *Theorem* denotes a proposition derived under an explicit proof calculus and is not a synonym for dialectical synthesis. Any unresolved historical source spelling is registered separately (§2.8 / Research Residuum); no new canonical logic element is inferred from it.

**Quality distinction.** Describe a property (*quality as attribute / characteristic*) separately from evaluating its goodness against criteria (*quality as assessment*). Criteria may be normative, ordinal, quantitative or qualitative. The Spanish `cualidad`/`calidad` distinction is a useful prompt for this separation but not a rigorous one-to-one normative/ordinal ontology. Expectation describes a goal; Satisfaction evaluates fulfillment under stated criteria.

### 5.8 Composition, Decomposition and Information Loss

Four different operations must not be collapsed:

| Operation | Formal/descriptive intent | What it **does not** imply |
|---|---|---|
| **Bidirectional navigation** | Inspect a directed relation from either endpoint while preserving its predicate/direction | A predicate is symmetric or has a logically valid inverse |
| **Composition / Decomposition** | Build a qualified pattern from documented pieces / follow retained references back to those pieces | A composite is invertible, a new predicate is proved, or missing pieces can be reconstructed |
| **Derivation** | Generate a new claim/value/view by an explicit, revisioned method from known inputs | Mathematical differentiation, guaranteed uniqueness or information preservation |
| **Projection / Aggregation** | Select, transform or summarize material for an observer/use | Every source element is included; reverse reconstruction from output is possible |

```text
COMPOSITION (partial, typed):
  asserted elements + typed qualified relations + method revision
    -> derived pattern with source/limitations links

DECOMPOSITION (documented traceability):
  pattern -> recorded constituents + original predicates + qualifiers

DERIVATION (method-bound):
  inputs + method revision + parameters -> output claim/representation

PROJECTION (possibly lossy):
  inputs + audience/selection/aggregation -> view + omission/loss descriptor
```

**Information preservation** is a separate, explicit property of a transformation: `lossless_within_declared_scope`, `lossy_with_declared_omissions` or `unknown/unassured` (illustrative vocabulary, **not** adopted technical enum). Source traceability can survive a lossy projection without restoring its missing data; a content fingerprint confirms identity of a *specified encoding*, not reversibility of a transformation.

**Composition is partial, typed and provenance-preserving.** Preserve endpoint types, predicate direction, scope, temporal validity, method/source revisions, trust and negative implications. `resultOf` must not become `verified`; `selected` must not become `admitted`; `related` must not become `causedBy`. Unsupported composition is **undefined**, not a negative empirical fact. This is compatible with the bounded Experiment 011 path construction, which proved neither universal predicate closure nor independent trusted premises (§5.11).

**Review consequence:** The formerly metaphorical names for the two navigation directions have no active operator status. Standard composition/decomposition terminology takes precedence, without claiming algebraic inversion.

**Parameter/revision is not effect (MR-03).** A changed parameter, profile, revision or encoding does not by itself establish changed Output, Observation or terminal Outcome. A claimed difference requires declared activation/preconditions, method/state revision and a supported effect path within the selected comparison scope. For example, changing retry backoff from 1 s to 10 s with exactly one attempt does not change that execution's retry behavior. Unsupported activation, feedback initial state/delay or solver/no-solution conditions remain undefined/unresolved; evaluation order must not manufacture a result.

### 5.9 Pattern Example — Experimental Decision with Feedback

1. A **Work Item** identifies the intended investigation; a **Hypothesis** is a qualified claim linked by Annotation/Evidence, **not** the same thing as its Conversation's Expectation.
2. A versioned **Protocol / criteria Artifact** declares trial conditions, controlled variables, variants and assessment method (proposed binding, not canonical Experiment object).
3. Two **bounded Executions** refer to the applicable version of that protocol; their **Events / Results** preserve performed-by, executed-as and resource/environment attribution to the extent available.
4. A comparative **Assessment / Conclusion** references the actual Evidence, limitations and criteria revision. An evaluation of a measurement is not an automatic Satisfaction judgment.
5. A **Selection** references the chosen variant and the supporting judgment. The subsequent **Action** requires the applicable Admission; Selection itself grants no permission.
6. Later **Results / Observations** may justify adjustment, refutation or a new experiment. Every canonical change follows admitted append-oriented recording and retained provenance.

This scenario is an expression test, **not** a claim of an implemented experiment engine. It tests whether each link can be addressed and qualified without inventing new canonical root entities.

### 5.10 Existing Ordering and Type Contracts

The X/Y/Z logical ordering of §3.2 is not a generic entity sorting contract. A proposed pattern may define local precedence (e.g. evidence evaluated before a derived judgment is promoted), but should not impose an unrelated total order on concurrent Events or force all actors through one sequence. Ordering, cardinality, type restrictions and allowed method composition are matters for a future **Relation Type / Pattern Contract** (§§7.16–7.18).

### 5.11 Formal Composition — Lessons from Experiment 011

A compositional framework needs **two distinct operations**: (1) path construction that preserves assertions; (2) inference under an explicit proof rule that may justify a *new* assertion. They cannot be conflated.

**Restricted typed-path model (observed in a finite experiment):** A path records its source/target, ordered asserted edges with individual qualifiers, compatible scope, temporal interval and provenance set. Two paths may concatenate only when endpoints and scope match and the interval intersection is nonempty. For this explicit construction, edge-list concatenation and provenance-set union are associative, while validity intersection uses `max(start)` and `min(end)`. Experiment 011 enumerated 216 three-edge interval cases, including 102 defined compositions and 114 undefined cases; these facts are **about the chosen finite path algebra**, not universal transitivity of AIAEAO predicates.

```text
path = (subject_ref, [qualified_assertion_1, ..., qualified_assertion_n], object_ref,
        scope_ref, valid_interval, provenance_set)

compose(p, q):
    require p.object_ref == q.subject_ref
    require compatible_scope(p, q)
    validity := intersection(p.valid_interval, q.valid_interval)
    require validity is not empty
    return path_with_concatenated_original_edges_and_preserved_qualifiers
```

**Preservation obligations:** Do not collapse `supports → selected → derivedFrom` into `causedBy`; never promote `selection` into `admission`, `result` into `verification`, or `reuse_eligible` into `execution_permitted`. A claim of transitivity, congruence, equivalence, probability combination or authorization requires its **own typed proof rule**. Even convenient qualifier aggregation may be invalid: unweighted pairwise averaging of three confidence numbers is generally non-associative. Unsupported compositions remain *undefined*, not false or silently allowed.

**Proof trace (candidate).** A proof object can cite named premises, versioned rules, steps and a conclusion; verification checks prerequisite presence and rule application. Its root premises require separate assurance. Experiment 011-C rejected eight authored forged traces and exhaustively evaluated 256 Boolean prerequisite combinations, yet did not establish truth of external observations or deploy a theorem prover.

**Temporal guard (candidate).** A certificate can be reused only under the applicable time, source/relation/method revisions and proof prerequisites. Actual execution additionally requires a **current** Grant/Authority check and Admission. Experiment 011-D produced counterexamples for omitted revocation, revision and expiry guards in deliberately defective finite models; its safe variant found no unsafe execution within the fully enumerated finite state space. This does not prove properties of an unbounded distributed runtime.

### 5.12 MEAS-01 / FUNC-01 (legacy M01/F01) — Admissible Measurements

**Separation of decisions:**

1. **Type/shape validity:** Do supplied values and their declared signatures match the function?
2. **Mathematical admissibility:** Are the operation-specific scale, unit, dimension, quantity and domain conditions satisfied?
3. **Epistemic warrant:** Is the empirical meaning and origin of the values justified by trustworthy, available Evidence?
4. **Governance admission:** Is any effectful invocation permitted for this Actor/Principal, resource and time?
5. **Assessment:** Does the result satisfy an explicitly versioned criterion? This is not implied by (1)–(4).

| Scale | Admissible re-encoding | Valid illustrative operations | Counterexample to reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal | Bijective/injective relabeling on observed classes | Equality, counts, mode | Mean of numeric category IDs |
| Ordinal | Strictly monotone positive order-preserving recoding | Ranking, ordered median | Arithmetic mean of priorities without a justified interval embedding |
| Interval | Positive affine (`a·x+b`) | Differences, affine-equivariant mean | Absolute Celsius-temperature ratio |
| Ratio | Positive rescaling (`a·x`) | Nonzero-denominator ratios; context-licensed sums/means | Treating all ratio values as additive, e.g. summing absolute temperatures without quantity semantics |

An output may change scale or shape: binning a metric into categories produces a nominal/ordinal view *only with defined thresholds and category semantics*; histogram counts may be ratio-scale but form a **structured output**, not a scalar. A valid arithmetic computation does not assert a Verified Conclusion, a Completed Contract or a Satisfied Expectation.

**Function discovery is not function execution.** A read-only `describe` query may require no execution admission, while invoking an effectful Tool always remains subject to existing Γ/Z governance. An indexed registry can improve lookup work but does not uniquely owe its speed to M01/F01: Experiment 012-C's equally safe indexed comparison used the same 32 catalog-entry visits as the typed-indexed approach.

### 5.13 Optimization Pattern — Experiment 010 as a Conditional Case

The synthetic 010 investigation compared **the same new worlds** under seven verification strategies. Its generator-specific `relation_rebind` locality contract supported additional negative `query.trans` certificates for unaffected non-root nodes; a typed impact qualification reduced modeled work. A shared-setup certificate bundle added a small central-case benefit. By contrast temporal index maintenance and adaptive selection did not outperform the strongest static design in the central profile.

**Permissible optimization rule (proposal):** Never infer that `references` alone proves absence of a dependency; skip a check only if an accepted locality and validity contract proves the omission safe for that particular predicate/method/version/scope. Count costs of type lookup, certificate creation, indexing, invalidation and switching; compare against an equally safe baseline with access to the same certificates. The result is *not* a universal speed claim for the morphology.

### 5.14 Type–Circuit Separation (Nonbinding Reference Patterns)

**Type/contract layer:** Specifies referent kinds, predicate signatures, units, time roles, origins, negative implications, permissions and validity conditions. It does not itself execute a policy or recompute a value.

**Circuit/control layer:** A versioned procedure checks a concrete request against current qualifiers and selects an authorized operation: e.g. `reuse`, `revalidate`, `recompute`, `fallback`, `refuse` or `admit`. Its operational effects and Evidence must flow through the existing X Γ / Ξ / Π and Z boundaries.

**Nonbinding engineering analogies:** A PWA/Service Worker suggests event-driven lifecycle, cache revalidation, fallback and version transitions; a firewall/policy engine suggests explicit gates, rule ordering and default-deny for effects. Neither technology, syntax nor runtime is mandated by the ontology. Cache freshness ≠ current Admission; *stale-while-revalidate* is not a permission to perform security-relevant actions under stale grants.

**Two kinds of negative rule:**

| Kind | Interpretation | Safe default |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition / operational gate | Requested effect is not authorized under current rules | `deny/refuse` until admitted |
| Non-implication / epistemic rule | Given premises do not justify a stronger inference | `not_established` / unresolved, **not** automatic refutation |

Illustrative pseudo-syntax (no parser or accepted enum implied):

```text
DENY execution WHEN current_admission != granted
DENY cache.reuse WHEN validity != confirmed
FORBID INFERENCE Result -> Verification
FORBID INFERENCE Capability -> Authority
DEFAULT inference = NOT_ESTABLISHED
DEFAULT external_effect = DENY
```

A **Cube Fingerprint** may be investigated as a fingerprint of a fully specified *relevant authorization/dependency context*. It is neither an accepted v9.1 canonical entity nor a proof that no asynchronous revocation occurred. Certified reuse requires authoritative, current invalidation/revalidation semantics and a safe time-of-check/time-of-use boundary. This is an **R-EXEC/R-DELTA experiment hypothesis**, not a production shortcut.

### 5.15 Deterministic Context Identity

A bounded **Context Descriptor** may be content-addressed when its fields, canonical serialization, references and relevant revision set are deterministically specified. This yields a reproducible **descriptor fingerprint**; the fingerprint can be used as a stable reference **to that description**, without introducing a new canonical root.

```yaml
# Illustrative descriptor / NOT an accepted persisted schema
context_descriptor:
  descriptor_schema_revision: 1
  scope_ref: thread:42
  protocol_revision_ref: artifact-revision:7
  criteria_revision_ref: artifact-revision:3
  trial_refs: [execution:101, execution:102]
  time_basis: recorded_as_of
  selection_method_revision: 1
context_fingerprint: hash(canonicalize(context_descriptor))
```

**Identity rule:** Same canonicalized bytes under the same algorithm/version give the same identifier; stable logical identity across descriptor/schema changes requires an explicit policy. Collision resistance and namespace/version binding cannot be assumed from a generic word `hash`. Ordered fields, unordered sets, external references, mutable targets, redaction, scope and known omissions need explicit canonicalization rules.

**Non-implications:** descriptor identity ≠ Execution identity ≠ actual delivery ≠ freshness ≠ Authority ≠ empirical truth. Two Runs may share a protocol/context description yet remain distinct historical Executions. If independent experiment lifecycle/invariants cannot be preserved with Work Item + Protocol Revision + Executions/Evidence/Assertions, the Class-C structural gate remains open (L04).

### 5.16 Measurement, Function and Comparison — Partial by Default

**MEAS-01** declares empirical feature interpretation, nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio scale, unit/dimension/quantity kind, shape, missingness, uncertainty and provenance. **FUNC-01** declares function signatures, domain, transformation preconditions, output qualifiers and method/implementation revisions. A **Conformance Context** checks whether a particular function is admissible for particular qualified values. Its identity can be specified as a versioned descriptive context without requiring a new X-Scope or Root.

A **Comparison Protocol** then separately defines alternatives, criteria, objectives, constraints, uncertainty policy and tie/non-comparability behavior. A numerical calculation does not choose weights, create evidence or establish a preference by itself. A measurement profile and function profile have **no mandatory one-to-one cardinality**; comparing two `(measurement, method)` combinations is a separate F method, not the implicit creation of `M02 ↔ F02` as canonical new types.

**Partial comparability is the default:** For qualified alternatives `a` and `b`, a declared comparison may return `a_preferred`, `b_preferred`, `equivalent_under_criteria`, `incomparable_under_criteria` or `undetermined_with_available_evidence` (conceptual outcomes; **not** adopted enums). Without an explicitly warranted total-order rule, a Pareto trade-off is not a forced ranking; weighting requires explicit criteria/source/version/authority. Equal assessment is not falsification, and incomparability is not equality.

Keep three judgments separate: *measurement assurance* (what did we measure?), *mathematical admissibility* (what can be computed?) and *decision evaluation* (which alternative satisfies a chosen objective?). Trust of input declarations, implementation equivalence, stochastic/numerical limits and conflicting metrics require further independent tests (R-MEAS).

### 5.17 Comparative Trust — Claim-Relative and Evidence-Bounded

A **Trust Assessment** is a qualified epistemic judgment on a **particular claim/measurement/assertion**, not an intrinsic, blindly inherited rank for its Source, Provider or Actor. It may compare provenance, attestation, temporal validity, method quality, evidence independence and stated limitations. A common possibly false source must not masquerade as multiple independent confirmations.

A comparison can express a supported preference, equality under stated criteria, incomparability or unresolved status. **Trust equality does not refute either claim**, including contradictory claims. A well-typed proof from unauthenticated premises remains conditionally valid but empirically unassured. Internal evidence may suffice for a **relative** comparison; it does not automatically authenticate external truth. Separate existing X Ψ judgments from X Γ Admission and Z permissions.

### 5.18 Experiment, Decision and Feedback Patterns

A research experiment may be described as a **qualified composition** of Work Item, versioned Protocol/Criteria Artifacts, bounded Executions, Events/Results, Evidence and Observation/Conclusion; no standalone `Experiment` root follows solely from its convenience. Distinguish an experiment **specification** (repeatable description), a historical **trial/run** (individual bounded Execution) and its **comparison/assessment** (method-/criterion-relative claim). A shared descriptor fingerprint does not merge separate Runs.

A **Decision Trace** distinguishes judgment → selection → current Admission → actual Action. A **Feedback Trace** distinguishes perceived/evaluated state → separately admitted adjustment → later result/observation; no implicit learning or privilege escalation. Dialectical **thesis/opposition/synthesis** remains a versioned candidate method; formal contradiction requires shared interpretation, scope and time, and synthesis is optional, neither a theorem nor verified automatically.

### 5.19 Partial Foreign Mapping and Semantic Loss

A foreign system maps through a versioned Adapter/Mapping Contract into existing AIAEAO referents. **Partial mapping is acceptable when its loss, unrepresented semantics, foreign references and limitations are explicit.** A mapping may be lossless in scope, lossy or partial; an end-to-end roundtrip is a separate reproducibility claim, not the definition of successful integration.

**Endogenous stability:** Foreign Actor IDs are not authenticated Principals; sampled/filtered foreign spans are not exhaustive Events; conformance/mining fit is not truth; X/Y/Z coordinates are not metric feature-space axes without a declared encoder. Unknown safety-critical identity, trust, ownership or authority information must block affected effects even if observation/import of other material is allowed. Existing canonical definitions cannot be overwritten by an adapter.

### 5.20 Self-Application of the Semantic Grammar — EXP-014-RMC

AIAEAO may **describe a change to its own definition** as Work Item, versioned Artifact/Revision, review, evidence, assessed Conclusion, authorized Selection, bounded Execution and admitted Event references without adding a new root. This is an **expressibility and traceability pattern**, not a global theorem of formal closure or a right to self-certify source truth (§§5.8, 6.7).

`EXP-014-RMC` tested eight authored scenario families across four recursion depths (0–3): **32/32** regular cases expressed, **12/12** authored negative mutations rejected. An alternative non-X flat process ledger also expressed **32/32**. A compressed representation retained source addresses yet could not regenerate missing source content. The positive conclusion is **bounded self-applicability with qualified provenance**, not exclusive expressive power, unlimited recursion, lossless projection or Class-C-root impossibility (`RES-RECURSION-01`).

### 5.21 Certified Relation Reuse Is an Algorithmic Choice — EXP-016-TRA-RC

Typed qualified relations, explicit evidence and shared dependency witnesses can support a **conditional optimization method**. The logical rule is: *skip/reuse only if the exact predicate-specific impact, trust root, revision, time, revocation, Scope and applicable effect boundary have been checked as required; otherwise safe complete evaluation*. This is a method/circuit precondition, not a consequence of using the X/Y/Z ontology.

`EXP-016-TRA-RC` compares five distinct strategies over the same synthetic workloads: **A Full Verified**, **B Safe Indexed**, **C Qualified Partial**, **D Shared Witness**, **E Independent Delta**. All five matched an independently implemented full oracle for **16,384 decisions each**, 0 false skips and 0 undetected false denials; **9/9 directed controls** and a separate post-hoc rebind/revocation sequence passed. D used **187.60 ms** over two aggregate runs versus B **284.69 ms** and E **300.04 ms** in the final exploratory benchmark. Runtime depends on method and workload: E's query path was much faster but had costlier updates; in an additional non-precommitted workload, E approached D. Both can satisfy the **same** safety contract. HMAC in this artificial world authenticates only its test key, not an independent trusted source. No exclusive AIAEAO speedup or real distributed safety is inferred.

### 5.22 Symbolic Grammar as Method — Nonimplications (v9.3)

Visual Grammar 0.9.3 follows the controlled pipeline:

```text
semantic ID / one X|Y|Z home
        -> canonical VG glyph (one Unicode codepoint)
        -> optional derived notation / form / color
        -> qualified SVG or UI projection
```

It is **not** the reverse pipeline: a glyph in an SVG does not reconstruct identity, provenance, authority or a valid result without explicit reference witnesses. The 26 separately reserved mathematical operators have established operand semantics and therefore receive **no mandatory color, form or enclosing shape**. A `⊢` written as a formal judgment remains a mathematical mark; X Authority is the separately registered pictogram **⬭**. The same holds for formal `⊨` vs the X Verification **⟢**.

**Visual taxonomic reuse** is permitted where canonical semantic identity and primary home remain stable. E.g. Γ Authority may be shown at a Z Project Boundary but is not thereby defined at Z. Glyph availability ≠ browser font fallback ≠ font-independent SVG. The 154 original SVGs remain historical, not a proof that their old labels are current.

The v9.3 symbol mapping incorporates **15 context-dependent review flags** (seven planning/resource applications; eight modules). They remain classified, traceable candidates, not 15 automatically approved ontology roots. This section does not define new runtime `mode`, `observer`, `proof_object` or fingerprint fields in §7.4.

## 6. Ontology

Ontology defines **what exists and what a term means**. Every canonical element is defined **once** in the X → Y → Z order fixed by §3.2. A term referenced in another axis retains its primary home; statements about its use there are **cross-references**. The axes are perspectives, not mutually isolated datasets. Technical identifiers, persistence shapes and exact enums start in §7.

### 6.1 Definition and Classification Discipline

Each entry is interpreted through five questions: *meaning*, *exclusion*, *identity/state*, *relationships*, and *authority*. Some elements are **objects**, others **endpoints**, **states**, **boundaries** or **projection-zone terms**. Classification under an axis does not force them into one physical storage representation.

| Primary home | Elements defined there | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **X: §§6.2.1–6.2.5** | 5 dimensions, Scopes and 10 Key Pair endpoints | semantic concerns and their logical judgments |
| **X: §6.2.6** | Logic Relations | rule/assessment connections across X |
| **Y: §6.3.1** | 6 Identity Anchors | stable identity and runtime-context binding |
| **Y: §6.3.2** | 7 resource-chain objects; Work Item; bounded Execution; Outcome; Annotation | identifiable, produced, intended, executed, terminal or interpretive referents |
| **Y: §6.3.3** | Relation Arrows | typed links between anchors and objects |
| **Z: §6.4.1** | Module Bodies | where activity is implemented or mediated |
| **Z: §6.4.2** | Boundaries including Project, Thread, capability limits, governance aspects | where ownership, authorization and operational effects apply |
| **Z: §6.4.3** | Resource Zones | where resources are situated or presented |

**Source-of-definition rule:** *Policy* and *Authority* have their meaning in X; in Z their **boundary application** is described without a competing definition. *Artifact/Evidence*, *Outcome*, *Annotation* and *Work Item* may be discussed by all X dimensions without changing their Y home.

### 6.2 X — Dimensions, Scopes, Key Pairs and Logic Relations

The five dimensions are ordered logically: Ψ → Γ → Σ → Ξ → Π. Each dimension has **exactly one Scope containing exactly one Key Pair**. A dimension heading is a reading home, not a canonical root object.

| Dimension | Scope | Key Pair | Distinct concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 𝝭 (Ψ) Epistemology | Observation | ⭔ Perception ↔ ⭓ Conclusion | support and justified judgment |
| 𝝘 (Γ) Governance | Policy | ⬭ Authority ↔ ⬬ Admission | permission and admitted request |
| 𝝨 (Σ) Semantics | Conversation | ☆ Expectation ↔ ★ Satisfaction | purpose and evaluation |
| 𝝣 (Ξ) Execution | Contract | ☐ Delegation ↔ ☑ Completion | responsibility and fulfillment |
| 𝝥 (Π) Provenance | Event | ⚐ Action ↔ ⚑ Result | attempted effect and recorded occurrence |

#### 6.2.1 𝝭 (Ψ) Epistemology

Epistemology asks what can justifiably be claimed from available records, and with what limitations. **Provenance identifies sources; Epistemology assesses what those sources justify**. Neither a model statement nor a high confidence score is canonical truth by itself.

**Scope — Observation.** An **Observation** is the conceptual frame for an Actor/system taking in records, results or artifacts and evaluating their support; it is not synonymous with a Provenance Event or with informal understanding.

**Key Pair A — Perception.** **Perception** is what is taken in, by whom/what and under which limits: available information, blind spots, filtering and possible uncertainty.

**Key Pair B — Conclusion.** **Conclusion** is a judgment warranted (or not warranted) by the available perceptions and supporting evidence. It may verify, refute or leave a proposition unresolved. It does not automatically confer policy authority, satisfy an expectation or rewrite the underlying Event.

**Relations.** Verification ⟢ and refutation ⟣ connect justified assessment to claims. Observation may reference a Y Evidence object or an Event Result in Π; such references do not turn those objects into new epistemic Key Pair members. See §6.2.6.

```text
Observation: Perception ↔ Conclusion
Conclusion != Satisfaction
Observation != justified Conclusion
```

#### 6.2.2 𝝘 (Γ) Governance

Governance asks who may do what, where and under whose authority. Its primary concern is authorization, not Visibility or runtime success.

**Scope — Policy.** **Policy** is the governing set of constraints, inherited defaults and explicit exceptions under which a request is evaluated. It need not imply a heavyweight permission matrix or forensic subsystem.

**Key Pair A — Authority.** **Authority** is the permission held by an Actor for an action over resources and scopes. Delegated Authority may be equal or narrower than the grantor's permitted extent; there is no implicit privilege escalation. Role may inform defaults but does not independently grant Authority.

**Key Pair B — Admission.** **Admission** is the decision for a *concrete requested action*: allowed or refused according to effective Policy, Actor/Principal context, resource and scope. It is a gate, not an assertion that the requested action succeeded. The explicit refused branch is represented by **Refusal** in Z's boundary vocabulary (§6.4.2).

**Relations.** Authorization ⬗ expresses that a request is permitted under governing constraints. Admission may constrain which Event or bounded Execution is admitted; Z supplies the Project/Thread scope, Grant, Capability Boundary and Execution Boundary. A visible item is not necessarily authorized; a technically possible Capability is not Authority.

Canonical conceptual rule: **no implicit privilege escalation**.

#### 6.2.3 𝝨 (Σ) Semantics

Semantics answers *what is intended and why*. **Work Item** is an independent Y object (§6.3.2), not a sibling of this dimension's Key Pair.

**Scope — Conversation.** A **Conversation** is a durable semantic object organizing information exchange around exactly **one active Expectation**. Messages and runtime sessions can participate but do not define the Conversation; a Conversation can span sessions. Boundaries may be declared, deterministically derived or inferred. Inferred boundaries require explicit uncertainty qualification, not silent promotion.

**Key Pair A — Expectation.** **Expectation** specifies what the Conversation intends to achieve. Its horizon may range from a milestone or story through a task to a diagnostic question or single-turn objective. Changes are linked by supersession or semantic relationships rather than destructive mutation.

**Key Pair B — Satisfaction.** **Satisfaction** is an assessment of how well that Expectation was met. Perspectives may be technical, economic, yield/value, risk, quality, usability or confidence; scales may be quantitative, ordinal, categorical or qualitative. Criteria can specify target, tolerance, weighting and measurement source. An aggregate score is derived, not automatic canonical truth. Satisfaction may be assessed after the Conversation or Thread closes.

**Relations.** Expectation and Work Item can refer to one another; supersedes/parent/child/related are relationship kinds whose technical spelling is given in §7. A satisfied human need does not follow automatically from a completed assignment or succeeded run.

```text
Conversation: Expectation ↔ Satisfaction
Completion != Satisfaction
```

#### 6.2.4 𝝣 (Ξ) Execution

Execution as an **X dimension** addresses assignment, bounded activity and fulfillment. It is distinct from the **bounded Execution record** defined under Y (§6.3.2). This removes the previous duplicate-definition problem.

**Scope — Contract.** **Contract** is one assignment of responsibility understood together with its fulfillment status. Here Contract means **Delegation ↔ Completion**, not a technical interface/schema contract.

**Key Pair A — Delegation.** **Delegation** assigns responsibility from a delegating Actor/orchestrator to a receiving Actor, with relevant Expectation, acceptance conditions, capability constraints, authority source and resource scope. A Delegation can reference a Work Item and one or more bounded Executions without owning those Y objects.

**Key Pair B — Completion.** **Completion** records whether the delegated assignment's acceptance/fulfillment conditions have been met. It is a responsibility judgment, **not** the terminal state of a particular run. A blocked or incomplete assignment may coexist with some successful bounded Executions.

**Relations.** A Contract may be fulfilled by accepted evidence and bounded Execution records. The bounded Execution's **Outcome** is *input to*, not proof of, Completion. Π provides Event/Action/Result records; Ψ provides verification/refutation where needed. A technical failure, denied request and unmet business purpose remain distinct.

```text
Contract: Delegation ↔ Completion
Execution (dimension) != bounded Execution (Y record)
Outcome != Completion != Verification != Satisfaction
```

#### 6.2.5 𝝥 (Π) Provenance

Provenance answers who/what supplied something, when and from which source, and what canonically occurred. The recorded chain is not identical to a justified Conclusion in Ψ.

**Scope — Event.** **Event** is the smallest append-only canonical occurrence/change record. Admitted Events retain provenance and support non-destructive correction, supersession and redaction, whose technical handling is specified in §7.

**Key Pair A — Action.** **Action** records an *attempted effect* on a system or environment: command, tool invocation, API request, file change, test or repository operation. CLI/CMD are channels, not canonical semantic root types.

**Key Pair B — Result.** **Result** records what an Action produced: exit code, response, report, diff, artifact reference, discovered issue or runtime metric. One Action may produce zero, one or many Results, which need not be terminal.

**Relations.** Result may be *resultOf* Action and link to Y Artifact/Evidence; Action can be *performed-by* Actor and *executed-as* Principal. Interpretation is carried by Y Annotation, including semantic classification, not by rewriting historical Event facts. Epistemic support is evaluated in Ψ; a bounded Execution terminal Outcome belongs to Y.

```text
Event: Action ↔ Result(s)
Event != Annotation; Result != Outcome
```

#### 6.2.6 X — Logic Relations

The complete X relation family is specified here, not hidden under Provenance or Epistemology:

| Relation | Symbol | Conceptual function |
|---|---|---|
| Invariant | ⟡ | condition intended to remain true |
| Constraint | ⬙ | restriction on admissible states/combinations |
| Compatibility | ⬘ | whether meanings/requirements can coexist |
| Authorization | ⬗ | whether activity is permitted |
| Verification | ⟢ | a proposition is supported under explicit checks |
| Refutation | ⟣ | a proposition fails the stated checks |

These are **logic/reading relations** from Visual Grammar. They do not automatically constitute new technical enum values. Verification and refutation are alternative judgments. Relation Arrows on Y (§6.3.3) and technical relationship types (§7.6) are distinct representations with different responsibilities.

### 6.3 Y — Identity, Identified Objects and Relation Arrows

Y answers *who or what is identified*. The three families follow the prescribed sequence: **Identity Anchors → Identified / Resource Objects → Relation Arrows**. Object relations do not turn one anchor into the parent definition of another.

#### 6.3.1 Identity Anchors

**v9.3 letter assignment:** Actor 𝕬 · Role Binding 𝕽 · Principal 𝕻 · Session Binding 𝕾 · Capability Profile 𝕮 · Adaptation Profile 𝕴. These are **visual type anchors**, not the stable IDs or the security principal itself.

##### 6.3.1.1 𝕬 Actor

An **Actor** is a stable identifiable participant capable of producing or receiving information, responsibility, authority, state or effects. Examples: human, AI agent, orchestrator, service, automation, tool proxy and external system. Actor identity is distinct from Role Binding, authenticated Principal, provider/model, runtime session and Capability Profile. Provider and model can change without changing semantic Actor identity where permitted by the relevant contract.

The Actor is the primary semantic subject of permission evaluation and of performed-by attribution. An Actor may hold multiple roles, operate through multiple sessions and expose capabilities/state without acquiring additional authority. All such associations are **bindings**, not nested redefinitions of Actor.

##### 6.3.1.2 𝕽 Role Binding

A **Role Binding** associates an Actor with a contextual responsibility under a specified scope and time interval. One Actor may be researcher for one Conversation, executor for one Execution and reviewer for another; several Actors may carry the same role. A Role Binding is not a mandatory Actor × Role × Scope permission matrix and does not by itself grant Authority.

##### 6.3.1.3 𝕻 Principal

A **Principal** is the authenticated technical identity under which an Actor or execution acts at a security/external-resource boundary. It supports **executed-as** attribution and Admission decisions. It may differ from the semantic Actor (*performed-by*). Principal is not a synonym for Actor, Role or permission grant. Its concrete credentials and fields belong to §7 and must not be embedded as secrets in Thread payloads.

##### 6.3.1.4 𝕾 Session Binding

A **Session Binding** connects a stable Actor to a transient runtime, provider, process or interaction context over a given interval. A session may support multiple Conversations; one Conversation may span several sessions. Session continuity does not prove canonical Thread completeness or persistent model memory.

##### 6.3.1.5 𝕮 Capability Profile

A **Capability Profile** declares which abilities a particular Actor, provider or runtime can technically support, for example session state, memory, skills, tools, subagents, structured output, streaming, tool calling, planning, parallel execution or context limits. This is an **extensible description**, not a closed canonical enum and not Authority. Actual availability can depend on the active runtime binding.

```text
Capability != Authority
Capability != Role Binding
Capability != Context
```

##### 6.3.1.6 𝕴 Adaptation Profile

An **Adaptation / State Profile** describes whether/how an Actor retains or changes state across interactions: stateless, session-bound, provider-managed memory, persistent agent state, skill state, adapted model or external state store. This is a declaration of capabilities and bindings, **not proof that the underlying model learned during the Thread**.

**AIAEAO-controlled Memory** is a derived layer under AIAEAO control (§4.3); **provider-managed Actor State** is external and may be opaque. Where external state influences a result, AIAEAO preserves attribution/available references, not a false claim of ownership or independent verification. Profile differences can inform context/delegation strategy without changing Thread semantics.

#### 6.3.2 Identified / Resource Objects

**v9.3 resource anchors:** Source ⍠ · Resource ⌹ · Content ⌻ · Revision ⍰ · Artifact ⌺ · Evidence ⌼ · Cache ⍞. The four additional defined Y referents are **Work Item, bounded Execution, Outcome, Annotation**; no new glyph is invented for the first, second or fourth. Outcome has a **provisional visual 𝕺**, never the X Conclusion ⭓. The 12 relation arrows, 24 historical states and 13 planning-symbol references in the Y glyph inventory are **not 49 additional ontology roots**.

The existing Visual Grammar resource chain is preserved in its order. The subsequent independent identified objects are added explicitly to give Work Item, bounded Execution, Outcome and Annotation **one definition home**. No extra X Scope or Key Pair is introduced. **Source qualification:** the v6 candidate enumerated several resource-chain terms but did not supply independent prose definitions for each; the concise descriptions below are **provisional conceptual explications of those existing terms**, not verified implementation semantics or newly approved schema elements.

##### 6.3.2.1 Source ⍠

A **Source** is a distinguishable origin of material or a claim. Provenance may reference Source to answer *from what*. Naming a source establishes attribution, not credibility or Verification. Concrete source identifiers, if stored, are a technical matter (§7).

##### 6.3.2.2 Resource ⌹

A **Resource** is an addressable item under ownership, permission and use constraints. It may be local, remote, logical or external, and may be associated with one or more locations/zones. Resource identity does not imply universal access.

##### 6.3.2.3 Content ⌻

**Content** is the material carried by a Resource or record, distinct from the Resource's identity and access boundary. Content may be unavailable, redacted or represented by a reference without erasing a resolvable canonical identity.

##### 6.3.2.4 Revision ⍰

A **Revision** distinguishes a version or changed state of referenced Content/Resource. It is not a reason to silently overwrite append-oriented canonical facts; version lineage may be related explicitly by technical references.

##### 6.3.2.5 Artifact ⌺

An **Artifact** is a durable output or referenced object produced or used by activity: file, diff, report, screenshot, test result or generated document. It can be used as Evidence but is **not automatically Evidence or canonical truth**.

##### 6.3.2.6 Evidence ⌼

**Evidence** is supporting raw material or a reference to such material used in assessment: logs, command output, tool payload, screenshot, test report, artifact or other record. It need not be fully embedded in the Thread. Evidence is a **role in supporting a claim**, and acceptance/Verification requires explicit assessment; mere existence is insufficient.

##### 6.3.2.7 Cache ⍞

A **Cache** is a retained, derived representation used for reuse, efficiency or presentation. Cache contents are not canonical authority and must preserve enough source/version information to avoid masquerading as original records. Invalid or absent cache must not silently invent facts.

##### 6.3.2.8 Work Item

A **Work Item** is a durable identified unit of **intended work** (*what is to be achieved?*). It may describe a milestone, story, task, bug, review or decision item. Work Items may have parent/child relations and be referenced by multiple Conversations, Delegations or bounded Executions. A Work Item is neither the Conversation Scope nor a third endpoint of Expectation ↔ Satisfaction.

##### 6.3.2.9 Bounded Execution

A **bounded Execution** is an identifiable unit of **performed work** (*what was actually done?*) that may reference Actors, Role Bindings, Delegations, Work Items, Conversations, Authority context, Resource/Environment Bindings, Events (Actions and Results), Artifacts and Evidence. It is an operational record associated with X's Contract concern, **not a second definition of the X dimension Execution**.

The execution is bounded by admitted permissions and applicable Execution Boundaries (Z), and its Actions/Results appear canonically as Event kinds (X Provenance). One bounded Execution has one terminal **Outcome**; a Contract may be evaluated over more than one such execution if allowed by its acceptance conditions.

##### 6.3.2.10 Outcome

An **Outcome** is the terminal state and evidence summary of **one bounded Execution**. It answers how that execution ended, **not** whether a Delegation was fulfilled or an Expectation satisfied. Examples of distinct terminal meanings: **denied** (authorization rejected the request), **blocked** (allowed in principle but currently unable to proceed), and **failed** (started but failed). Other representative states include succeeded, cancelled, timed out and partial.

Outcome may summarize duration, compute/token use, monetary cost, resource use and supporting Evidence references. It is **grounded by provenance-bearing Results and diagnostics** where available; it neither takes the place of those Events nor performs Verification automatically. The relation is:

```text
Event Action(s) + Result(s) (Π)  ── support / reference ──▶ Outcome of Execution (Y)
                                                          │
                                                          ├── supports assessment of Completion (Ξ)
                                                          ├── can be evaluated under Conclusion (Ψ)
                                                          └── can inform Satisfaction (Σ)
```

Outcome is a terminal **state of the bounded record**, not necessarily an independently identified root entity. Its primary semantic definition nevertheless remains here.

##### 6.3.2.11 Annotation and Semantic Classification

An **Annotation** is an interpretation, classification, validation or evaluation **of an explicitly referenced object or record**. It does not silently rewrite the referenced canonical fact. Its origin (declared, observed, derived, inferred or adjudicated), reviewer/Actor, confidence where meaningful and possible validation state keep interpretation distinguishable from occurrence.

**Claims and hypotheses (D.4).** A qualified claim/hypothesis about a referenced subject is represented as a Y Annotation; a freestanding thesis/text may be Y Content. Asserting it is a Π Event; assessing its justified validity belongs to Ψ Conclusion; its conversational context is Σ Conversation. These are distinct roles, not a new Claim root. Addressing `Y:α:Annotation["claim:A"]` requires an actual locator and declared binding; this text does not invent one. Where several assertions share one attachment, independent revision/correction of one assertion still needs a separately specified addressability contract (RES-REL-01 / L01). That contract remains open; the illustrative separate Annotation locators do not prove it exists in runtime.

**Semantic Classification** is a classification assigned **through an Annotation to a referenced object** (for example a Conversation, Event, Result or Content). Labels such as discussion, planning, execution, review, diagnostic, meta or unknown are classification values, not additional ontological root objects. Low relevance can likewise be qualified without deleting a canonical record.

```text
Referenced canonical object
       ▲
       │ target/reference
   ANNOTATION
       ├── classification (optional semantic label)
       ├── origin / method of attribution
       ├── confidence / limitations (when appropriate)
       └── reviewer / validation (when known)
```

**Annotation belongs to Y as an interpretable identified attachment; its event of creation, when canonical, belongs to Π.** Epistemology evaluates whether the interpretation is justified. The exact implementation may represent it as a record or metadata reference; this chapter does not mandate a new storage root.

#### 6.3.3 Relation Arrows

Relationships connect identities and objects without embedding a second object definition. The Y Visual Grammar arrow inventory remains:

| Arrow | Symbol | Directed meaning (conceptual) |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | ↣ | chosen object/reference |
| Reference | → | points to another referent |
| Uses specification | ⇢ | uses a description or contract |
| Admission / refusal | ↪ / ↛ | admitted or rejected linkage |
| Performed-by | ↠ | Action → Actor |
| Executed-as | ⇛ | Action → Principal |
| Production | ⇥ | produces output |
| Observation | ↝ | observes or takes in a referent |
| Causation | ⇰ | triggered/caused by another occurrence |
| Derivation | ↦ | derives from prior material |
| Projection | ⇉ | appears in a derived view or zone |

The arrows describe **semantic connections**. Technical relationship kinds, event correlation and causation are specified separately in §§7.6–7.7. Some arrow symbols are visual only; never infer a schema field or permission from the glyph.

### 6.4 Z — Materialization & Boundary

Z is organized into **Module Bodies → Governance & Boundary → Resource Zones**. It gathers three kinds of location or extent: implementation placement, authoritative boundary and presentation/resource zone. They share a spatial question but **are not homogeneous storage containers**.

#### 6.4.1 Module Bodies

These elements classify **where functions are located or mediated**, rather than defining new canonical X Key Pairs or Y resources. The ordered vocabulary is descriptive unless a specific technical binding is approved in §7. **Source qualification:** v6 defines Core/Connector/Toolbox ownership but largely enumerates the remaining body names; their short functional glosses here are provisional interpretation, not claims of already implemented modules.

| Body | VG glyph | Conceptual responsibility / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** | 𝗞 | canonical model stewardship and admitted ingestion; only the Core admission path changes canonical state |
| **Connector** | 𝗖 | run control, technical observation, execution diagnostics and mediated interactions; not a direct canonical writer |
| **Toolbox** | 𝗕 | capability parameters, normalized results and supporting Evidence content; not a direct canonical writer |
| **Tool** | 𝗧 | an invocable operational capability or method reached through an appropriate boundary |
| **Adapter** | 𝗔 | maps an external/runtime vocabulary into AIAEAO semantics without redefining them |
| **Integration** | 𝗜 | a coordinated connection to another software/service boundary |
| **Runtime** | 𝗥 | the operational context in which an Actor/tool is actually served or executed |
| **Provider** | 𝗣 | service/model/infrastructure source implementing a runtime or capability |
| **Third-party Dependency** | 𝗗 | externally owned component whose reliability, limits and authority cannot be assumed |

The sequence is a **reading direction from core to external dependency**. It does not assert that each body strictly contains the next. Ownership is differentiated: Core controls canonical ingestion; Connector and Toolbox provide operation and evidence, not unmediated canonical writes.

#### 6.4.2 Governance & Boundary

The family is read from wider boundary to narrower effect. Where a term was defined in X, this section specifies only **its Z boundary application**.

**Boundary ⬠.** A boundary marks an extent across which identity, permission, effects, provenance or representation need explicit rules. Not all boundaries are physical containers.

**Project ⬕.** **Project** is the administrative ownership, default-permission and namespace boundary in which Threads and Resources live. It supports owner/group, inheritance and future derived-view/Memory scope. The Project is not a sixth dimension or a Conversation.

**Thread ⬖.** **Thread** is a durable semantic and provenance boundary for a coherent objective space. It is neither a transcript, runtime session, workspace, single task, memory store nor monolithic storage blob. A Thread can associate objective, Conversations, work, Actors, Events, outcomes, authority and evidence while preserving references. **RAW means canonical, minimally interpreted, loss-aware and reconstructable — not unstructured.**

A **Relay Thread** is persisted **coordination** context, distinct from a **Canonical Thread** admitted under this model. Neither a runtime conversation nor Relay summary becomes canonical truth through persistence alone. The method of explicit promotion is in §5.4; technical stores and boundaries in §7.5.

**Policy ⬥.** Z locates where the X Policy (§6.2.2) applies and how defaults inherit across Project, Thread and narrower resources. It does not create a second Policy object.

**Authority ⬭.** Z locates the resource/scope for the X Authority (§6.2.2). Actor and Principal are separate Y anchors; permissions follow the applicable source and scope, not the mere possession of a Role or Capability.

**Grant ⧨.** A **Grant** is an explicit bounded conferment of permission from a valid authority source. Grant scope cannot silently exceed the grantor's authorized extent. A Grant's authoritative implementation and reference are specified in §7, not implied by a UI setting.

**Admission ⬬ / Refusal ⧩.** These denote the admissible/rejected branches of a concrete permission request at a relevant boundary. **Admission** retains its X definition (§6.2.2); **Refusal** is the negative result of that same decision, not an extra X Key Pair endpoint. Neither proves a runtime Outcome.

**Capability ⬒.** A **Capability** is something the acting runtime/tool can technically perform. It may be described by a Y Capability Profile, but does not constitute Authority. Z concerns *where* its use is permitted or constrained.

**Capability Boundary ⬓.** A **Capability Boundary** restricts which available abilities may be reached within a particular context, independent of whether an Actor has a broad capability profile.

**Execution Boundary ⬔.** **Execution Boundary** constrains the effects of an already permitted action: sandbox, workspace, filesystem/network scope, credentials-by-reference, tool access and external-service reach. It is distinct from the earlier authorization question: **may the Actor act?** vs. **how far may this admitted action reach?**

**Visibility ⧊.** **Visibility** determines whether authorized information is surfaced to an Actor, view, Context Builder or audience. It may inherit defaults and be narrowed on specific objects. **May read ≠ automatically shown**. Its primary Z home is the presentation/exposure boundary; the underlying permission test remains in X Governance. The concrete visibility values are listed with technical identifiers (§7.14).

**Ownership and inheritance** generally propagate Project defaults down to Thread and narrower work/resource scopes with bounded overrides; inheritance is defaulting, not irreversible copying. A denied Actor does not gain implicit administration privileges.

#### 6.4.3 Resource Zones

These terms organize **placement and presentation**, not new independent canonical truth stores. Their one-sentence descriptions make explicit the v6 naming scheme; they remain **provisional descriptive meanings** until the model and Visual Grammar are jointly accepted:

| Zone / marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ownership ⬦** | which scope controls/possesses a resource; cross-refers to Project/permission boundary |
| **Resource Zone ⬧** | identified operational or presentation zone for a resource |
| **Back Office ◾** | internally oriented operational/technical activity and evidence handling |
| **Front Office ◻** | mediation and preparation of information or interaction for consumers |
| **Front Stage ◽** | user/observer-facing appearance of selected information and controls |
| **Zone Boundary ⬪** | distinction between zones, possibly with access/transfer rules |
| **Same Resource Across Zones ⬨** | one canonical identity appearing in multiple zones without duplication of truth |

A zone label describes a **position or projection**. It does not grant access, duplicate the Resource or replace permission checks. The observable consequences of these zones are covered in §4; physical routing and binding, where implemented, belong to §7.

### 6.5 Qualified Relations and Temporal Facts — Ontological Status

Neither **Typed Relation** nor **Temporal Qualification** is introduced here as a new canonical root entity. They describe **ways of stating, qualifying or evaluating relationships between existing X/Y/Z referents**. Every referent keeps its one primary definition home. Where a relationship assertion itself requires stable addressability for correction, contradiction, verification or historical review, its technical representation may use an existing Event / Annotation / Relationship identity or an explicitly reviewed extension (§7.16).

| Analytical construct | Ontological interpretation | Primary definition homes involved | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relation predicate | Meaning of a connection | X Logic Relations or Y Relation Arrows; technical mapping in §7 | A semantic glyph is not a schema enum |
| Relation assertion | Claim that a specific predicate holds between referents | Admitted Event / referenced Annotation, depending on kind | Assertion ≠ fact established / verified |
| Temporal qualification | Named time role attached to a referent or relation | Existing X/Y/Z definition home | `referent + role + value` required to disambiguate |
| Method / protocol | Versioned procedure or evaluation specification | Y Artifact / Revision, referred to from other homes | Referenced protocol ≠ protocol applied |
| Hypothesis / opposition / synthesis | Qualified claims or interpretive stages in a pattern | Ψ Observation/Conclusion and Y Annotation/Evidence | No automatic theorem, Verification or acceptance |
| Decision trace | Judgment, selection and possibly admitted effect | Ψ Conclusion, Y relation, Π Action, Γ Admission | One judgment is not an authorization |
| Feedback trace | Qualified causal and assessment links across observations and adjustments | Ψ / Π + Y / Z | Observation and modification remain separate |
| Quality attribute | Property of a subject, without judgment of goodness | Subject's ontology home plus qualified Annotation if needed | Characterization ≠ evaluation |
| Quality assessment | Judgment under criteria and Evidence | Σ Satisfaction / Ψ Conclusion / Y Annotation | Assessment ≠ intrinsic property |

**Not adopted as new roots:** `Experiment`, `Decision`, `Feedback`, `TemporalFact`, `RelationAssertion`, `Quality`, `Theorem`, or `Axiom`. These may be **method labels, assertions, artifacts or future design candidates**, not independent canonical definitions by mere mention. The model does not prohibit such entities forever; §7.21 records criteria that could justify them.

### 6.6 Primary-Home Preservation and Cross-Axis Traversal

A relation may span dimensions, but **must not move its endpoints**. For example:

```text
Ψ Conclusion [X] --supportsSelection--> selected variant / Artifact [Y]
Γ Admission  [X] --permits in scope--> Action [X/Π] under Execution Boundary [Z]
Π Result    [X] --resultOf--> Π Action [X]
Y Cache     [Y] --derivedFrom--> Y Revision [Y], qualified by validity time [T]
```

These lines describe semantic traversals and **proposed** predicate uses; they are not a declaration that the written identifiers are accepted technical relationship kinds. A cross-axis assertion with an X logic predicate may be evaluated through rules rather than stored as a Y relationship edge. An actor- or audience-specific projection is a phenomenological use, not a second definition home.

There is **no requirement for a symmetric pair per Y anchor, Z module or temporal facet**. X alone retains exactly one Scope/Key Pair per its five canonical Dimensions. Pair, triad, n-ary comparison and cycle are legitimate **method shapes** when their semantics warrant them.

### 6.7 Evidence, Authority and Negative Space

Ontological expressibility does not authorize execution or prove truth. A qualified predicate must preserve these non-equivalences:

```text
Reference != Selection != Application
Mentioned specification != Applied specification
Observation != Perception != Conclusion
Hypothesis != Expectation
Claimed opposition != Logical contradiction
Synthesis != Proof / Verification
Judgment != Admission != Action
Occurrence time != Recording time != Assessment time
Persisted bytes != Canonical promoted truth
Cache exists != Cache is fresh != Cache is authoritative
Result != Outcome != Completion != Satisfaction
Actor's capability != Actor's authority
```

The original nomenclature's non-equivalences (§2.3) remain authoritative. Additional operator names should enter canonical vocabulary only if existing definitions cannot preserve a necessary distinction; otherwise they remain qualified aliases or method terms.

**v9.1 Type–Circuit separation:** `FORBID INFERENCE` expresses absence of inferential warrant, not a claim of falsity; an operational `DENY` applies to effectful activity until a current admissible authorization is established. These are **distinct rule families** despite their common negative-space purpose (§5.14).


### 6.8 Research Boundary of the Ontology

### 6.10 Qualified Contexts Are Not New Roots

Context Descriptor, Context Fingerprint, Context Assembly, Conformance Context, Comparison Protocol, Ordering Specification, Trust Assessment and Mode/Observer Perspective are **qualified descriptions, methods, or judgments anchored in existing X/Y/Z homes**. This document does not promote them to canonical root types. A stable deterministic locator may address a description or relation assertion without creating a new ontological category.

**Class-C gate retained:** Only demonstrable loss of independent identity, lifecycle, authority or invariant when using existing Event/Annotation/Artifact/Revision/Relationship/Tool and bounded Execution referents may justify a new Root. Current review agreements are a **conceptual disposition**, not proof of concrete serialization, source trust or external implementation.

## 7. Topology

> **TECHNICAL REFERENCE FRAME BEGINS HERE.** §§1–6 define the conceptual model and its presentation/logic. From this point onward, the document specifies canonical **technical representation, persistence, IDs, relationships, integrity and versioned invariants**. The meanings of conceptual terms are not redefined here; consult their single primary home in §6.

The source v6 identifies a PROD-capable technical target, with POC/MVP/BETA as subsets of the same model. This inherited v7 technical reference, retained in v9/v9.1, does **not** itself freeze a schema. Fields marked `code` are candidates for freezing **upon acceptance of the applicable schema version**; implementation-specific lists remain explicitly qualified.

### 7.1 Canonical Technical Binding Map

The technical realization binds the identified X/Y/Z concepts without turning the logical nesting in §5 into a physical record tree.

```text
PROJECT  [Z: administrative namespace, ownership and default governance]
   │
   ├── admitted governance / resource / environment bindings
   │
   └── THREAD  [Z: semantic + provenance boundary]
         │
         ├── append-only EVENT envelope(s)  [X: Π, Action ↔ Result]
         │     ├── actor, scope and correlation references  [Y / Z]
         │     ├── conversation / work / execution references  [X / Y]
         │     └── artifact / evidence references  [Y]
         │
         ├── referenced conversations / expectations / assessments  [X: Σ]
         ├── referenced delegation / completion / bounded execution  [X: Ξ, Y]
         ├── referenced observation / claim assessments  [X: Ψ]
         └── provenance-aware relationships, annotations, projections
```

This is a **binding overview**, not a claim that every conceptual object is implemented as a root table or that each Thread physically owns every resource. Only **admitted Core ingestion** changes canonical state; Connector and Toolbox may observe, normalize or forward but cannot bypass that boundary.

### 7.2 Project: Namespace and Ownership

A Project provides administrative ownership and namespace for Threads and associated resources, with at least owner and primary group concepts. Technical bindings must permit default permissions, visibility, resource scope, Thread creation and derived-view/Memory scoping without elevating denied participants implicitly.

**Security baseline:** deny by default; grant explicitly or through controlled inheritance. An unauthorized Actor should receive an explicit permission conflict rather than implicit administrative privileges. Project's conceptual definition remains in §6.4.2.

### 7.3 Resource Ownership and Inheritance

Defaults inherit from **Project → Thread → associated Work Item / Conversation / bounded Execution / Artifact / Event** unless a permitted override narrows or changes effective policy. This is **default resolution, not an irreversible value copy**. The same mechanism may later apply to Memory, derived knowledge, consolidated views and external Resources.

Technical authorization must keep Actor, authenticated Principal, applicable Grant, action and resource scope distinguishable. Role Binding and Capability Profile may inform evaluation but must not alone confer Authority. Visibility and allowed execution effects remain separate decisions.

### 7.4 Event Envelope

An admitted Event should support at least the following candidate technical fields, preserving the v6 intent:

```text
event_id
project_id
thread_id
timestamp
sequence / local ordering
actor_ref
kind
payload
schema_version
correlation_id
causation_id
references
provenance
visibility
```

Optional references:

```text
conversation_ref
work_item_ref
execution_ref
delegation_ref
role_binding_ref
artifact_refs
authority_ref
extensions
```

These are **candidate identifiers and slots**, not a JSON Schema supplied by this document. A serialized Event must distinguish Action/Result kinds (where used) from outcomes, annotations, derived observations and semantic labels. For example, `kind: action` with a shell channel does not make `CMD` or `CLI` a top-level ontological object.

### 7.5 Thread and Relay Persistence

Canonical Thread data are **append-oriented, loss-aware, minimally interpreted and reconstructable**, not one opaque storage blob. The persistence model must preserve sufficient connections among objective, expectation, responsibility, admitted actions/results, actors, resource/cost evidence, completion, satisfaction, chronology, causality and authorization context.

**Relay Thread** is a different persistence/coordination context from the **Canonical Thread**. No automatic import, migration, synchronization, bridge or adaptation of legacy Threads is required or authorized by this model. Promotion of runtime/Relay material into accepted durable project authority uses explicit source, review, authorization and qualification (§5.4), not direct equivalence of storage records.

### 7.6 Relationships and Reference Integrity

Relationships are first-class and provenance-aware; the preserved v6 technical relation vocabulary is:

```text
parent
child
references
related
sameAs
supersedes
derivedFrom
causedBy
resultOf
fulfills
```

Inferred relationships must retain distinguishable origin and confidence where available. A relationship may connect relevant canonical objects without asserting technical containment. Cross-axis referents must retain stable identity and ownership boundaries.

**Non-equivalences at the interface:** `fulfills` or an Outcome reference does not by itself prove Completion; `resultOf` is not Verification; `sameAs` must not erase identity/provenance differences; a semantic Classification changes neither Event kind nor canonical occurrence.

### 7.6.1 REL-01 — Typed Relation Contract (integrated candidate; legacy R01)

**Target:** enable a typed, qualifier-preserving interpretation of existing direct references, Y relation semantics and X logical connections **without** imposing a universal physical edge table or new canonical root. This is a design candidate, not a frozen schema.

#### 7.6.1.1 Abstract signature

```text
QualifiedRelation :=
  subject_ref       : canonical or otherwise qualified reference
  predicate_type    : stable, namespaced semantic predicate identifier
  object_ref        : canonical or otherwise qualified reference
  qualifiers?       : scope / temporal / origin / evidence / method / status / limits

RelationTypeContract :=
  predicate_type
  semantic_definition + negative_implications
  allowed_subject_kinds / allowed_object_kinds
  direction / optional inverse / symmetry semantics
  cardinality and relevant scope
  permitted + required qualifiers
  admissibility and provenance conditions
  composition rules / forbidden inferences
  technical realization mapping (if any)
  revision / authority for contract changes
```

`QualifiedRelation` and `RelationTypeContract` are **conceptual types**. They may be realized using existing `*_ref` slots, relationship kinds, versioned Artifact specifications or admitted assertion-bearing Events as appropriate. The proposed shape is deliberately independent of one storage technology, URI convention, enum or schema revision.

#### 7.6.1.2 Predicate groups, without collapsing distinct logics

| Group | Examples | Realization / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference and identity** | `references`, `sameAs`, `parent`, `child` | Existing technical relation vocabulary, with provenance constraints |
| **Production and causation** | `resultOf`, `causedBy`, `derivedFrom` | Existing; do not infer causation from time adjacency |
| **History and fulfillment** | `supersedes`, `fulfills` | Existing; fulfillment link alone does not prove Completion |
| **Contextual semantic relations** | Selection, Uses Specification, Observation, Projection | Candidate type/qualifier mapping, not accepted new enum names |
| **Logical predicates** | Invariant, Constraint, Compatibility, Authorization, Verification, Refutation | X logic **judgments**, not automatically persisted edges |
| **Temporal predicates** | before, during, valid-until, recorded-at | Candidate qualifications; not automatically distinct entity kinds |

**Do not treat** a direct `actor_ref`, a visual arrow, and an epistemic verification as interchangeable just because each can be rendered as a graph edge. Mapping must name its loss, qualifications and authority source.

#### 7.6.1.3 Worked binding options

Example: *Execution 42 used Revision 7 of Protocol P*, allegedly supported by an admitted Result.

```yaml
# Illustrative notation; not a canonical AIAEAO event or JSON schema
subject_ref: execution:42
predicate_type: example.usesSpecification
object_ref: artifact-revision:7
qualifiers:
  origin: observed
  evidence_ref: event:103
  scope_ref: thread:8
```

Option A: a direct `protocol_revision_ref` slot is suitable where the semantics are invariant and locally scoped. Option B: a qualified relationship is suitable when multiple methods, time conditions or alternative claims coexist. Option C: an addressable assertion linked to an admitted Event/Annotation is suitable when that **specific claim** must be corrected, refuted or superseded. **Options are not mutually exclusive**; serialization is a separate engineering decision.

**Correction rule:** An inferred selection or claimed `usesSpecification` must remain identifiable as an assertion; correction appends a new qualified assertion or status, never silently changes the meaning of the original evidence. A technical relation may carry its own stable identifier or be addressable through `(event_id, local_assertion_key)` if the latter demonstrably meets reference-integrity requirements. Neither form is mandatory here.

#### 7.6.1.4 R01 acceptance criteria

| ID | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| R01-AC01 | Subject and object references resolve under applicable ownership and visibility rules; predicate meaning is unambiguous. |
| R01-AC02 | Subject/object kind constraints, direction and required qualifiers are machine-checkable for a declared Relation Type Contract. |
| R01-AC03 | Scope, temporal role, origin, Evidence and limitations remain distinguishable in storage or through resolvable links. |
| R01-AC04 | A specific relationship assertion can be corrected, rejected or superseded without unexplained provenance dead ends. |
| R01-AC05 | Composition cannot implicitly confer Authority, Verification, Completion, Satisfaction or causal certainty. |
| R01-AC06 | Existing direct references and established technical relationship kinds continue to work; no automatic legacy migration. |
| R01-AC07 | Derived patterns can be navigated back to their **documented** constituent referents, assertions, revisions and limits. |


### 7.7 Correlation, Causation and Ordering

Parallel activity needs more than timestamps. Use `correlation_id` to group a logical activity, `causation_id` to link a triggering prior record, and timestamp/sequence or parent/child execution structure to express available order. The model supports **partial ordering**, not a fabricated single total order.

Parallel branches and delayed results must remain distinguishable. Technical ordering metadata must not imply causal relationships when only chronology is known.

### 7.7.1 TIME-01..06 — Temporal Bindings and State Reconstruction (integrated candidates)

**Profile boundary (MR-01/MR-04/MR-05).** Clock origin/unit/transform, validity, uncertainty and method revision belong to an explicitly versioned T profile. Payload/time equality never substitutes for source-scoped occurrence identity. Observer scope, sampling, gaps and freshness remain declared separately; unknown/partial coverage cannot establish a complete history. These are profile obligations, not new Event fields or a replacement for the selected public contract. MR-02 remains at REL-01; MR-03 at PAT-*/FUNC-01; MR-06 is a scope-bound assessment obligation. See [composition alignment](MILE-071-COMPOSITION-ALIGNMENT.md) for bounded proposed homes and dispositions.

The technical Event envelope in §7.4 already proposes `timestamp` and sequence/correlation/causation references. **Do not retroactively reinterpret that field** without migration/version review. For any additional temporal role, prefer a typed qualifier whose semantics include the referent and its role.

| Candidate contract | Smallest expressible requirement | Source element / pending decision |
|---|---|---|
| T01: Time role | distinguish occurrence, recording, perception and assessment | Event/Observation/Conclusion; time-source and precision qualifiers |
| T02: Effective interval | express start/end of Role, Grant or Policy applicability | Existing Y identity binding and X/Z governance |
| T03: Cache validity | link cache to source revision(s), freshness criterion and invalidation predicate | Y Cache/Revision/Resource, derivation relation |
| T04: Historical view | retrieve recorded state `as_of` and explicitly distinguish retrospectively obtained evidence | Canonical Thread + temporally qualified Context Assembly |
| T05: Temporal dependency | distinguish order, causation, deadlines and revalidation triggers | `causation_id`, `sequence`, constraint and time role |
| T06: Runtime / persistence | identify ephemeral context, durable relay and admitted canonical state by boundary and lifecycle | Existing Session, Runtime, Relay/Canonical Thread; no extra storage-specific domain |

**Minimum counterexample:** An external action occurs at T1, its result is received at T2 and a conclusion is assessed at T3. One unqualified `timestamp` cannot simultaneously stand for all three. Another counterexample: a policy with validity ending at T2 does not necessarily govern a later review of an action that occurred at T1. Both cases require explicit time semantics, **not** new time entities by default.

A cache may be **present but stale**; recording a source revision is necessary for some invalidation methods but not alone sufficient for all TTL, remote dependency or non-versioned external-source cases. The selected freshness rule and unverifiable dependencies must remain visible.


### 7.8 Resource / Environment Bindings

Environment is **not required as a new mandatory canonical root object** in this version. Project and bounded Execution can reference resource/environment bindings such as local, staging, production, workspace, repository, filesystem, network, credential set and external service.

The source v6 illustrates different permissions by environment (e.g. local read/write/execute versus production read-only); the example is **not** an operative policy. Actual permission and execution bounds must be evaluated against the effective Actor/Principal, resource scope and applicable grants. Credentials/secrets are referenced, never embedded as Thread payloads.

### 7.9 Redaction / Deletion Without Provenance Dead Ends

If canonical content is redacted or physically erased, retain resolvable identity and reference state where possible. One possible *illustration*, not a universal schema:

```text
event_id: E42
state: redacted
payload: unavailable
reason_ref: R7
```

Corrections, supersession, reassessment, validation or classification changes append new records rather than silently rewrite historical facts. **Deletion must not produce unexplained provenance dead ends.**

### 7.10 Tamper-Evident Provenance

Tamper-evident provenance remains a **post-PROD strategic target**, not a blocking requirement for first production iterations. Preserve its prerequisites now: stable event IDs, append-only semantics, immutable admitted payloads, causation links, local sequence information and explicit correction/redaction records.

Possible future mechanisms include event hash chains, signed checkpoints, Merkle structures, external attestation and immutable storage; none is silently declared implemented by this document.

### 7.11 Foreign-System Interoperability

External concepts map into AIAEAO canonical objects via adapters and **namespaced extensions**, without redefining internal ontology. Representative namespaces from the source:

```text
extensions:
  github.*
  grok.*
  herdr.*
  vendor.*
```

GitHub Agent Sessions, Grok Build child sessions, and Herdr dispatch/pane/agent runtime can be mapped, as warranted by evidence, to AIAEAO Delegation, bounded Execution, Actor and Resource references. The mapping is not an assertion of semantic equivalence, authority inheritance or available provider capabilities.

### 7.12 Design Rules / Technical Acceptance Principles

The source v6's design rules are retained and qualified by the v7 classification discipline. Most rules are **constraints on technical realization** rather than additional ontology definitions.

1. **Project is the administrative / ownership boundary.**
2. **Thread is the semantic / provenance boundary.**
3. **Execution Boundary is the operational security boundary.**
4. **A Thread is not a storage blob.**
5. **RAW means canonical, loss-aware, and minimally interpreted — not unstructured.**
6. **Thread, Conversation, Execution, Actor, Memory, Context, Authority, and Visibility are distinct concepts.**
7. **Every Conversation has one active Expectation.**
8. **Perception ↔ Conclusion, Authority ↔ Admission, Expectation ↔ Satisfaction, Delegation ↔ Completion, and Action ↔ Result remain separate key pairs.**

### 7.12.1 PAT-* — Pattern / Protocol Binding (integrated candidate family)

A composite method may refer to a **versioned protocol** (initially a Y Artifact with Revision), its permissible input/output roles, parameterization and validation evidence. A method signature could be expressed as:

```text
PatternContract:
  pattern_type / revision
  named_participant_roles       # role requirements, not automatic ontology objects
  required_relation_predicates  # their Relation Type Contracts
  qualifiers / temporal requirements
  entry and admissibility conditions
  output claims and their evidence limits
  permitted side effects / required admission checks
  failure, refusal, unresolved and partial paths
  provenance / replay expectations
```

No `Pattern` root table, mandatory workflow engine or method-execution machinery is implied. A Pattern Contract may be documentary, an artifact, or a future machine-validated rule package. Preserve distinct operational provenance for the **execution of a method** and epistemic support for **claims produced by that method**.

| Composite | Minimal referent chain | Proof obligation / open binding |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | Conclusion → Selection → Admission → Action | Selected option and selection basis must be distinguishable from mere reference; admission is independent |
| Dialectics | Claims with opposition/compatibility → candidate synthesis | Unresolved conflict permitted; synthesis provenance and warrant required |
| Experimentation | Hypothesis + Protocol Revision → bounded Executions → Results/Evidence → Conclusion | Trial conditions, measurement units, control/variant and criteria must be traceable |
| Feedback | Observation → Evaluation → admitted Adjustment → Result → renewed Observation | Separate observation, authorization, action and effect; no automatic learning inference |
| Projection | Thread + perspective/visibility + method → derived view | Provenance-preserving loss disclosure and non-authoritative projection |
| Promotion | Candidate + review/evidence + Authority → admitted durable material | Durable bytes ≠ authority; explicit authorization / qualification required |

**Experimental gap test.** Before introducing an `Experiment` entity, attempt to express all trial comparisons using `(Work Item, Protocol Artifact Revision, Executions, Evidence, Assertions)`. Only an unresolved identity/lifecycle/invariant failure can motivate a structural extension.

### 7.13.1 MEAS-01 — Measurement Scale Contract (integrated candidate profile; legacy M01)

**Goal:** Define and check the interpretation, not just representation, of quantitative/qualitative attributes. `M01` names a candidate **logical profile** over existing versioned Artifact/Revision, Resource/Content/Annotation/Evidence and R01/T bindings. It is not asserted to be an independent canonical root or mandatory public service.

```yaml
# Illustrative registry specification — not an accepted AIAEAO schema
feature_spec_ref: artifact-revision:feature_cost_3
subject_kind: execution
value_semantics:
  scale: ratio                     # nominal | ordinal | interval | ratio
  value_shape: scalar             # scalar | vector | series | distribution | histogram | ...
  value_type: decimal
  empirical_definition: "modeled work per event"
  unit: synthetic_work_unit
  dimension: modeled_work
  quantity_kind: additive_cost     # not implied by scale=ratio
  domain: {minimum: 0}
qualifiers:
  measurement_origin: synthetic_generator
  revision_binding: generator-revision:009
  time_role: assessed_at
  limitations: ["not observed wall-clock duration"]
```

**Proposed minimum semantic fields** (some may be expressed via references instead of literal fields):

| Component | Must preserve | Negative example |
|---|---|---|
| Feature identity and empirical definition | What property of which subject is asserted; applicable revision and scope | Arbitrary nominal code treated as magnitude |
| Scale / ordering / admissible transformation | Nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio and applicable transformation family | Unlicensed mean of ordinal priority codes |
| Unit / dimension / conversion context | Compatibility of units and arithmetic result | EUR + seconds; cross-currency conversion without rate/time provenance |
| Quantity kind / additivity / zero semantics | Whether summation, differences and ratios carry domain meaning | Sum of absolute Kelvin temperatures as an asserted physical aggregate |
| Value shape / cardinality / missingness | Scalar versus vector/series/histogram; null/censored/unknown meaning | Count histogram presented as a scalar |
| Precision / uncertainty / sampling | Error, rounding, coverage, bias and known limitations | Sampled data presented as exhaustive ground truth |
| Time / version validity | Applicable observation, method and source revisions; expiry/invalidations | Expired evidence used as current input |
| Origin / attestation / trust | Who supplied the empirical declaration; evidence for its correctness | Nominal codes falsely declared as ratio numbers |

**Validity relation:** A checker can validate *declared metadata consistency* without authenticating the declaration. M01 must not claim to infer the true scale from values alone. Missing or unverifiable provenance must remain epistemically visible. Source measurement, modeled quantity, simulation and prediction remain distinct modalities.

**Required acceptance scenarios:** M01-A1 positive nominal mode and ordinal median; M01-A2 reject nominal numeric average and unjustified ordinal arithmetic; M01-A3 positive affine/ratio metamorphic invariance where specified; M01-A4 reject Celsius absolute ratio; M01-A5 check dimensional conversions and quantity-kind additivity; M01-A6 detect invalid interval/source/method revisions; M01-A7 reject scalar/histogram shape confusion; M01-A8 flag unverified declarations instead of asserting their empirical correctness. Experiments 012-A and stress probes provide only a bounded subset of these criteria; A8 remains unresolved rather than passed.


### 7.13.2 FUNC-01 — Function / Formula Contract and Registry (integrated candidate profile; legacy F01)

**Goal:** Make declarative mathematics discoverable, typed, composable and separately executable when authorized. An F01 entry is a **versioned method/formula specification**, typically a Y Artifact with Revision, with an optional Z Toolbox/Tool implementation. A formula expression is not a trusted implementation or a derived theorem; its execution is not automatically permitted by discovery.

```yaml
# Illustrative F01 specification — not an accepted AIAEAO schema
method_id: statistics.weighted_mean
method_revision: 1
kind: formula
inputs:
  values:
    type: numeric_series
    scales: [interval, ratio]
    shape: vector
    unit_policy: same_convertible_unit
  weights:
    type: numeric_series
    scale: ratio
    shape: vector
    dimension: dimensionless
preconditions:
  - equal_lengths
  - weights_nonnegative
  - sum_weights_positive
  - values_quantity_kind_allows_mean
formula_expression: "sum(w[i] * x[i]) / sum(w[i])"  # interpreted only by a declared parser
output:
  scale_policy: preserves_compatible_input_scale
  unit_policy: same_as_values
  shape: scalar
execution:
  effect_class: pure               # separate authority required for effects
  implementation_ref: toolbox:mathematics/weighted_mean
  numeric_precision_policy: declared_by_implementation
provenance_policy: retain_input_revisions_and_method_revision
```

**Three separate collection roles:**

| Library kind | Stores / exposes | Runtime requirement | Existing home |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formula Library | Declarative expressions, definitions, references, proofs/assumptions where available | None merely to browse a formula | Y Artifact/Revision; F methods |
| Function Library | Callable implementations plus signatures and test/conformance evidence | Z Tool/Runtime only for invocation | Z Toolbox/Tool; Y Artifact contracts |
| Method / Protocol Library | Composition, criteria, variants, failure and authorization conditions | May be purely documentary | F Pattern, Y versioned Protocol Artifact |

**Registry exposure (proposed):** An Artifact index in Project context can enumerate methods by namespace, purpose, signature, method revision, availability/implementation and provenance. Optional adapters map foreign libraries. Its query interface should support `describe`, `list`, `match(signature)`, `explain_rejection`, and, subject to admission, `invoke` without conflating discovery and execution. The registry is a **derived index**, not authoritative simply because it exists in cache.

**Composition contract:** Output signature of operation A must satisfy input signature, dimensional requirements, time validity and shape of operation B. Record method revisions, input-artifact revisions, actual parameter values/derivation, precision/error budget, and when appropriate a proof/verification trace. Stochastic methods require distribution, randomness/seed, independence and sampling assumptions as appropriate; absence of these metadata prevents claims of reproducible equality. A formula result may become Evidence for a Conclusion under explicit evaluation; it is not itself a Conclusion, Completion, Admission or Satisfaction.

**Discovery / execution authority:** The ability to find or describe a function grants no capability to execute a remote tool. `Capability ≠ Authority`; policy/Grant/Admission and execution boundary must be checked using the applicable Actor/Role/Principal and time. Declaring `effect_class: pure` in a third-party manifest alone is not an authenticated safety guarantee.

**Required acceptance scenarios:** F01-A1 reject incompatible input types/scale/unit/shape; F01-A2 validate method revision and domain preconditions; F01-A3 compose a valid formula chain while retaining input/method revisions and qualifications; F01-A4 reject expired/stale inputs; F01-A5 return no usable method when a name matches but contracts fail; F01-A6 describe method without granting effectful execution; F01-A7 validate implementation equivalence, precision and error on real tools (open); F01-A8 apply current governance and provenance against real Core admissions (open).

### 7.15 Technical Scope and Operational Statement (Inherited)

AIAEAO's technical target is a **project-scoped, append-oriented, provenance-preserving representation** of human and machine work, binding semantics, responsibility, execution, evidence, authority, visibility and epistemic status without collapsing their meanings.

The core should support the question:

> **WHO DID WHAT WHEN WHY — under which authority, with which result, at what cost, on what Evidence, with what confidence, and how satisfied are we?**

The technical boundary is explicit: **admitted canonical records and versioned contracts start here**; observer-specific projections remain derived, and foreign systems enter through qualified adapters. A v7 documentation candidate is not itself a schema freeze, acceptance decision or evidence of implemented runtime behavior.

### 7.19 Cross-structure Mapping and Ordering

The three relation surfaces must have a **documented mapping** rather than a forced one-to-one technical type mapping. At minimum a mapping records the source semantic predicate, target technical representation, type restrictions, qualifier retention and known information loss.

Current reading orders are explicitly defined in §3.2. The model also requires **partial temporal ordering** for parallel executions (§7.7). Neither rule authorizes a universal sequence over all entities. The following ordering concerns are separate:

1. **Definition order**: stable primary home, controlled by the model.
2. **Logical/method order**: local constraints and typed relation dependency, controlled by method/logic contracts.
3. **Historical order**: recorded/occurred/validity roles with partial causality; not inferred from simple display position.
4. **Projection order**: user/view preferences that may change freely subject to visibility and provenance.

A generic saved user-sort/order contract is not presently specified. It should be introduced only when required by real navigation or persistence use cases.

**v9.1 descriptive `Ordering Specification` candidate:** A presentation order may declare `sort_by`, direction, grouping, tie-breaker and applicable view/context revisions. This is not a replacement for logical precedence, causal partial order or definition ordering, and **not an accepted mandatory event-envelope field**. Pure visual preference may be ephemeral; decision-relevant order must be reconstructably referenced (§4.9).

### 7.38 Shared Evaluation Gates for Integrated Candidates

This section is a **cross-candidate checklist**, not a duplicate technical specification of REL/TIME/PAT/MEAS/FUNC.

### E.3 Distinctions that experiments did not eliminate

- **Expression versus conformance:** An R01 relation might be writable in a proposed schema but impossible to reconstruct from real foreign-API evidence.
- **Declared measurement semantics versus measured reality:** Accurate values do not certify that feature descriptions, category ordering or unit conversions were honestly declared.
- **Proof trace versus proof of true roots:** A trace checker can accept a logically valid derivation from false or outdated premises.
- **Temporal guard versus distributed safety:** Finite single-clock executions do not model races, clock skew, partial orders and provider-managed hidden state.
- **Typed method matching versus optimal discovery:** Indexing quality, search cost, safety of manifests and tool latency are separate concerns.
- **Local certified skip versus general cache reuse:** Experiment 010's shortcut was tied to special `query.trans` generator conditions, not arbitrary LLM answers.
- **Mathematical result versus governance:** A correct computation or valid proof never by itself grants Authority, Admission, Completion or Satisfaction.
